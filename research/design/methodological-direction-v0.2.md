**Date:** 14 November 2026  
**Researcher decision:** Model C (*capability-in-practice*) selected as the preferred direction in conversation. **This is not approval of a finished method or ethics amendment.**  
**Sources:** the original `practice-as-software-research-plan.md` (researcher-supplied, approved IIPP LREC plan), [conceptual review](../../wiki/synthesis/conceptual-framework-v0.1.md), [18-paper synthesis](../../wiki/synthesis/new-postgraduate-evidence.md), and [10-claim full-text audit](../methods/ten-claim-evidence-audit-2026-11-09.md).  
**Register:** version 0.2 is a human-readable methodological **working discussion** for researcher review. It does not authorise new data collection.

## 1. Central question

**Model C working question, selected by researcher:**

> When and how do students make, scrutinise and justify consequential policy-analysis choices in AI-supported practice, and what evidence, if any, suggests change in that capability over time?

This builds from the original approved question:

> How does interaction with policymemo.ai affect the development of students' policy reasoning, and under what conditions does that interaction strengthen or weaken independent analytical judgement?

The Model C wording avoids requiring the study to demonstrate causal effects before its method can warrant them. However, **'change over time' remains a distinct and relatively demanding inference**, and should not be claimed solely from a good post-AI memo.

**Plain language:** we want to understand how students make significant decisions while tackling policy problems; what they do when they encounter AI advice, peer challenge or uncertainty; how they explain their choices; and whether there is convincing evidence that their judgement develops during the course.

**Terminology decision:** do not call the object of observation an ‘episode’ in the research plan or participant materials. The previous `episode-analytic-framework-v0.1.md` is a technical exploratory schema, not agreed methodology. Prefer **'how students make and revise significant policy decisions'**, **'changes in their reasoning'**, or **'the development of their policy analysis'**. A working analytical unit can be a *documented decision or revision*, without foregrounding that label in prose.

## 3. Where the literature requires a methodological progression

### A. From *better answers* to *the quality of reasoning*

**Original plan:** compare problem framing, evidence, uncertainty, policy options and judgement before/after use.

**Progression:** retain those five domains, but ask in each: **what was the student's initial reasoning, what changed or remained the same, and what reasons supported that choice?** A student is not rewarded merely for changing their answer or disagreeing with AI.

**Evidence:** [Tai, C09](../../wiki/sources/tai-2018-evaluative-judgement.md) and [Bearman, C10](../../wiki/sources/bearman-2024-evaluative-judgement-ai.md) help define judgements about the quality of work and AI-mediated processes. These sources are conceptual, **not empirical proof that a specific intervention increases learning**.

### B. From *AI feedback provided* to *what the student does with it*

**Original plan:** note where students accept, adapt, reject or contest the tool's reasoning.

**Progression:** examine the **grounds for the response** and visible subsequent work. Sound acceptance may indicate good judgement; automatic rejection does not. Distinguish 'AI gave a critique' from 'student evaluated the critique' and 'student revised the policy analysis'.

**Evidence:** [Elkhodr & Gide, C03](../../wiki/sources/elkhodr-gide-2026-ai-critic.md) make acceptance, modification and rejection visible in scaffolded postgraduate work; [Farrokhnia, C04](../../wiki/sources/farrokhnia-2026-feedback-revisions.md) demonstrate that more highly rated feedback did not yield significantly stronger revisions. Do not infer durable capability.

### C. From a linear before/after comparison to *reasoning across people and tools*

**Original plan:** pen-and-paper work, AI, then pen-and-paper; peer work already exists in teaching.

**Progression:** preserve a short record of the **initial individual reasoning**, what was challenged or introduced in **group discussion**, the contribution of the **AI**, and the **student's later position and justification**. The methodological question is not just *did it change?* but *what can we actually trace about how and why?*

**Evidence:** [Herdağdelen, C02](../../wiki/sources/herdagdelen-experts-judgement.md) suggests studying upstream analytical choices rather than only final recommendations, but its artificially leant agent and non-significant adjusted final policy effect limit direct transfer. [Dai et al., C06](../../wiki/sources/dai-et-al-2026-cognitive-outsourcing.md) describe different trajectories in student **groups**, not the causal effect of AI on individuals.

**Validity caveat:** the present approved sources may **not** capture informal group discussion or who first proposed an idea. Unobserved contributions must remain unknown. Structured individual observation is a **proposed** additional method requiring an ethics amendment.

### D. From visible reflection to evidence of *development*

**Original plan:** compare later final memos and ask whether changes 'last into later work'.

**Progression:** distinguish:
1. **Assisted work:** what the student produces using peers, teachers or AI;
2. **Judgement exercised:** evidence that they compare, question, evaluate or justify choices;
3. **Development over time:** evidence that they can use relevant judgement in later work or on a comparable challenge, with assistance and task conditions made explicit.

**Evidence:** [Wiles, C01](../../wiki/sources/wiles-2026.md) and [Fernandes, C08](../../wiki/sources/fernandes-2026-metacognition.md) show why apparent performance and perceived mastery need separating. [Zou, C05](../../wiki/sources/zou-2026-doctoral-writing.md) complicates simple AI-harm/benefit narratives.

**Validity caveat:** the final memo extends the same project and may still use AI. This is **continuity of practice**, not independent transfer. A new later comparable individual task would strengthen that claim, but would need methodological and ethical justification, not be introduced by stealth.

## 4. A practical research sequence to explore, not yet an approved research task

**Existing teaching idea (refined):** students work on the same policy problem in four stages:

1. **Before the discussion:** write an initial individual position and why it seems defensible (for instance, problem definition; evidence gaps; preferred criterion).
2. **After working with peers:** note a challenge or alternative and whether it affected their position.
3. **After PolicyMemo.ai:** identify the most consequential AI claim or question, and how they assessed it—not simply whether they agreed.
4. **Back to individual work:** explain the position they now hold, why, what remains uncertain, and whether they rejected, modified or retained earlier ideas.

This is a **candidate refinement of normal teaching**, not a preapproved research-only worksheet. Requiring students to write justifications can itself shape what they do; where possible, compare material that did not explicitly prompt a rationale. If a modified activity becomes a new research procedure, seek approval before collecting or analysing new data.

**Example:** a student initially attributes low benefit uptake to lack of awareness. Peers question that assumption; AI proposes administrative burden as a competing account. The later student work weighs evidence for both. What matters is the **substantive appraisal and justification**, not how many statements were changed.

## 5. What the available / proposed data can actually tell us

| Material | Reasonable inference | Cannot automatically infer | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Optional existing seminar work | How a student's documented policy position and rationale changed within normal work | Why unrecorded thoughts or conversations changed | Original approved plan; consent-dependent |
| Voluntarily shared, redacted tool conversations | What PolicyMemo.ai actually suggested; visible student responses | All AI use, tacit reasoning, peer influence | Original approved plan; consent-dependent |

| Structured classroom observations | Richer information on social reasoning and division of work | Unobservable thoughts or independent competence | **Proposed amendment** |

| New comparable individual follow-up task, if deemed necessary | Stronger evidence about later independently exercised judgement | Causal effect of AI without further design safeguards | **Not yet in agreed plan; may require amendment** |

No student research material can be processed in an LLM under the originally approved plan. The repository can hold the methodology and synthetic examples, **not participant data**.

## 6. A lean qualitative analysis rather than a ten-part coding machine

Retain the original five domains of policy reasoning—**problem framing, evidence, uncertainty, options and judgement**. For each significant change or decision **visible in authorised material**, record:

- **Initial position** and grounds (if available);
- **Subsequent advice/challenge** from group, tool or teaching (only when visible);
- **Decision or revision** (including defensible non-change);
- **Student's grounds and uncertainty**, separating displayed explanation from analyst inference;
- **Alternative explanations and missing evidence**.

This is a **working comparison structure**, not a fixed score or a validated psychological instrument. Metacognition and reflective practice can *sensitise questions* about planning, monitoring and reframing. They should not be used to assert what someone was privately thinking.

Analyse across individual cases and different activities; deliberately seek **negative cases**: well-justified AI agreement, poorly justified human-first claims, weak AI criticism that a student rejects, a polished memo with no observable rationale, and substantive reflection without changing the conclusion.

A second human coder can critically compare interpretations of a selected set of authorised texts, consistent with the original approved plan. We should specify how disagreements are resolved before analysis.

## 7. What should change in the eventual research plan?

**Retain:** the module setting, genuine policy task, individual/group/AI sequence, voluntary material, qualitative approach, researcher–teacher safeguards, and original five analytical domains.

**Clarify:** a research question focused on how students *make, evaluate and justify* choices; distinctions between tool output quality, enacted judgement and development; the exact source and limitations of each observation.

**Propose in the subsequent ethics amendment:** richer structured observations, carefully timed interview accounts (six to eight), potentially expanding groups, and refined seminar activities justified by what they make observable. Separate ordinary teaching changes from research-specific collection.

**Hold open:** whether the study genuinely has sufficient evidence to claim *development* over time. If not, make **exercise/justification of judgement** the defensible primary result and describe later change cautiously.
