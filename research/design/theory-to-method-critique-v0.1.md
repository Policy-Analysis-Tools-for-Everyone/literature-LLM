# Conceptual framework → methodology: adversarial review v0.1

**Date:** 14 November 2026. **Status:** research assistant's critical challenge, not human peer review or an ethics decision. Based on [framework](../../wiki/synthesis/conceptual-framework-v0.1.md), [audited claims](../methods/ten-claim-evidence-audit-2026-11-09.md), and the [provisional episode guide](episode-analytic-framework-v0.1.md).

## What would each theoretical approach actually predict or notice?

| Model | What it privileges | What would make it more convincing | What would weaken it |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Individual evaluative judgement | Person's ability to apply quality criteria and justify policy choices | Comparable individual tasks over time; justification unprompted by an AI rubric | Good judgement only inferred from polished final output; no evidence of student contribution |
| B. Situated collective practice | Tool, pedagogy, people and routines shaping policy reasoning | Observable sequence of peer/tool/teacher moves, plus artefact changes | Only individual final memos and incomplete chat logs; organisational strategy concepts transfer without justification |
| C. Capability-in-practice (working candidate) | Quality of judgement *as exercised* through situated episodes, with development as separately assessed | Episodes expose grounds for decisions and whose contributions are visible; later individual work provides cautious temporal contrast | A collection of overlapping labels with no discriminating observation; claims development from prompted activity alone |

## Main conceptual challenges

**A capability is not identical to an episode.** A decision episode can show application of standards but may miss tacit knowing, historical learning and non-verbal group negotiation. Absence of visible reflective language must not be scored as absence of judgement.

**Metacognition is not a synonym for quality.** Frequent checking can be misguided; automatic but well-founded decisions may be skilful. Score the *quality and relevance of reasons* separately from whether a check was written.

**Reflective practice is not ‘I reflected’.** Schön's original work needs closer reading before interpreting an edited statement as reflection-in-action. Separate a documented reframing occasion from an unobservable private cognitive event.

**Strategy-as-practice may be the wrong scale.** Jarzabkowski et al.'s praxis–practices–practitioners vocabulary makes social action visible, but taught MPA policy analysis is not necessarily organisational strategy work. Retain as a rival lens; seek genuinely policy-practice literature if it does more explanatory work.

**Prompting is treatment.** A seminar that instructs students to accept/reject AI advice and provide a rationale **changes the behaviour being observed**. Comparing it with an unprompted baseline is a design consideration, not a neutral observation.

## Rival explanations to document

- Existing expertise, motivation, English-language proficiency or AI familiarity;
- Tutor/peer discussion or curriculum differences;
- Task repetition, assessed work incentives and rubric exposure;
- AI model selection, prompt wording, agent skill and differing access to tools;
- Participation and consent self-selection; after-marking delay;
- Selective sharing of successful interactions or retrospective account;
- Researcher–teacher dual role; potential pressure, reactivity and demand effects.

## What the current data can and cannot support

**Permitted source categories under the original plan:** opt-in seminar work, voluntarily shared conversations, eligible final memos after marking and non-identifying teaching notes. These can support descriptive analysis of visible framings, revisions, criteria and occasional traceable sequences, subject to consent and approved UCL handling.

**Cannot establish from those alone:** that the AI caused change, that a group move was an individual's thought, that the absence of logged checking means no metacognition, or that a final memo improvement equals retained independent ability.

**Amendment proposals only:** structured classroom observation, interviews, recruitment beyond two groups and new research questionnaires. Draft these only after method choice; do not collect data under the existing approval.

## Proposed methodological direction for researcher evaluation

1. Specify **one primary outcome**: how evaluative judgement is *exercised and justified* in policy decision episodes, with quality defined using domain-specific, contestable criteria.
2. Keep **development** a separate, more demanding claim: require temporal comparison of genuinely comparable individual work, ideally with less scaffolding or a clear account of tool access. If unavailable, narrow the claim rather than exaggerating evidence.
3. Use the episode schema to analyse **visible processes**, with uncertainty and provenance coded honestly.
4. Test whether group dynamics can be analysed under the permissions and material actually available; otherwise restrict claims to recorded, voluntary episodes.
5. Employ an explicit negative-case strategy: sound AI acceptance, ill-founded AI rejection, unchanged-but-defensible position, improved output with weak rationale, reflective talk without substantive revision.
6. Evaluate whether **six to eight proposed interviews** would fill an actual inferential gap (why a decision was made), without treating retrospective recall as direct proof of moment-to-moment cognition.
7. Seek feedback from an independent methods reviewer before drafting the amendment.

## Decision gate

The framework and codebook are **provisional**. To adopt them, the researcher must resolve (i) central construct, (ii) how development is identified or bounded, (iii) what unit of analysis can be observed ethically, and (iv) why a practice lens improves the research beyond description.
