# Teaching design v0.5 — two core PolicyMemo.ai seminars

**Date:** 14 November 2026. **Status:** proposed study design, not retrospective account of sessions delivered and not new ethics approval. **Researcher direction:** two or three seminars with the AI tool, not AI in every seminar. Working recommendation: **two core sessions**, with a third only if justified later.

## Rationale

The study examines policy judgement in practice, not AI use for its own sake. **Non-AI activities are valuable in their own right** and show how students respond to tutor feedback, evidence and peers. The AI sessions add distinct challenges within this broader course. Participation in teaching and access to PolicyMemo.ai remain open irrespective of research consent; an equivalent peer critique route remains available.

| Teaching opportunity | Role of AI | Core educational purpose | What research *might* learn, only from consented/authorised data |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Moore's Strategic Triangle** — first core tool session | After initial individual thought and peer deliberation, PolicyMemo.ai acts as **critical reader** of how the student's policy choice balances public value, legitimacy/support and operational capacity | Weigh competing public values and institutional constraints; defend the reasoning behind an option | Which criteria students prioritise; whether AI points change how they weigh feasibility, legitimacy or public value; reasons for retaining or revising a position |
| **B. Thinking Hats / policy memo speed dating** — second core tool session | **Only after successive human peer-feedback rounds**, a short PolicyMemo.ai challenge of the emerging memo/pitch (or specific argument), then individual response | Judge feedback from different lenses: facts, caution, logic/process, narrative, strengths and alternatives | How students evaluate conflicting peer and AI suggestions; what they retain, adapt or reject and **why** |
| **Formative feedback and AI decisions** — no live tool required | Discuss AI practices and decision-making **without** requiring a new interaction | Interpret tutor feedback on the actual formative problem definition, discuss evidence and questions with peers | Judgement of educational feedback and AI's role as described by students; **not evidence of an observed AI intervention** |
| **Imperfect evidence exercise** — third core AI session **only if later warranted** | Conditional critical challenge to an initial evidence-based recommendation; must follow human analysis | Deal with competing evidence and uncertainty | May add something distinct about **calibration and uncertainty**, but use only if the first two leave a real research gap |

### Small common documentation structure — proposals for next delivery

Each core tool seminar can retain a **short** individual starting position; group/peer challenge; a bounded AI critique; an individual closing position with an explanation. Students may **defensibly keep their original view**. Do not score how many changes they make.

For research, record only the sources actually available: an initial and later student artefact, any independently consented voluntary AI conversation and, **if approved**, an observer's factual notes. The teaching task can have identical materials for all students, while research collection remains entirely optional.

The **Thinking Hats** source deck currently tells students to incorporate a piece of advice each round. **Suggested improvement:** invite students to *accept, adapt or decline* advice **with reasons**, without rewarding agreement/revision. Doing so changes the activity's measurement characteristics: students are prompted to justify, and this must be recognised in interpretation.

### Research questions across the sessions

- **Strategic Triangle:** “When a policy choice involves conflicting criteria, how does the student decide what matters most?”
- **Thinking Hats:** “When exposed to multiple critiques, what does the student judge to be valuable and why?”
- **Non-AI formative work:** “How do students assess tutor feedback and their own evidence before or aside from direct tool interaction?”

**Do not** frame non-AI seminars as a randomised control group; session topic, teacher, task and ordering differ.

### Practical timing constraints

The source deck names Thinking Hats as **Week 8**, while Strategic Triangle appears in the earlier module seminar plan. At 14 November 2026, some earlier sessions may already have happened. We cannot retroactively make these prospective observations. This two-seminar programme describes the **planned amended/future delivery**. For the present cohort, map **actual dates and what was really delivered**, then identify any eligible ordinary teaching artefacts under the original consent; any newly structured research collection must await approval.
