Practice as software

Semi-structured interview guide — Interview 2
Participants: Consenting IIPP0010 postgraduate students
Timing: Following completion of the module and release of final grades
Duration: Approximately 30–45 minutes
Interviewer: Christopher Rosselot, Juan Pablo Carbonell Muñoz or Maria Nieto Rodriguez

Purpose: Explore how students’ approaches to policy reasoning and AI-supported work have
continued or changed since completing the module, including how they exercise judgement when
approaching a new policy problem.


Six core questions
1. Looking back at IIPP0010, what stands out about how you approached your Personal Policy
Problem and how your thinking developed during the module?

2. Since completing the module, have you approached any academic, professional or personal projects
differently as a result of what you learnt?

3. Has the way you use AI changed since the beginning of the module? How do you decide when to
use it, what to ask it to do, and what you would rather work through yourself?

4. Thinking about your experience with policymemo.ai, was there anything that influenced how you
evaluate evidence, question assumptions or make decisions? Has any of that stayed with you?

5. Can you describe a recent situation where you had to make a difficult judgement or work through
an uncertain problem? How did you approach it, and what influenced your decisions?

6. Looking back, do you think your ability to make and justify policy decisions has changed? What do
you think contributed to that, and is there anything about your approach that you would now
question or do differently?


Optional follow-up policy task
Participants who have separately consented will complete a short, unfamiliar policy-analysis exercise.
Afterwards, the interviewer may invite them to discuss how they framed the problem, weighed
evidence and criteria, handled uncertainty, and whether or how AI might have helped. The task is
ungraded and separate from the interview.


Interviewer guidance
Use the questions flexibly and encourage concrete examples, including those that challenge
assumptions about AI’s benefits. Distinguish the influence of policymemo.ai from teaching, peers,
other AI tools and professional experience. Confirm consent and optional Microsoft Teams audio
recording before beginning; participants may skip questions or stop at any time. At the close, remind
them that Naeha Rashid (naeha.rashid@ucl.ac.uk) manages consent and withdrawal requests.
