Overview
Title of study: Practice as software: exploring the use of AI in policy reasoning and critical thinking

Supervisor: David Eaves, Associate Professor and Co-Deputy Director, IIPP — d.eaves@ucl.ac.uk

Details of Research
We would like to invite you to take part in a study of how students use policymemo.ai, an AI policy-
analysis tool, in IIPP0010 seminars. We want to understand how students make and justify choices
about policy problems while working independently, with peers, with tutors and with AI, and
whether aspects of their approach change later in the module or after it ends.

Please take time to read the following information carefully and discuss it with others if you wish.
Please ask if anything is unclear or you would like more information.

What is the project’s purpose?
This research project is led by Jack Strachan, a PGTA on IIPP0010, and sponsored by David Eaves, the
module lead. Jack created policymemo.ai independently of UCL.

Students and public servants increasingly use generative AI for policy analysis. We want to
understand what this means for students’ own reasoning: how they assess evidence and feedback,
respond to challenges, consider policy options and justify their decisions. We will consider both what
students produce and how they describe and demonstrate their reasoning. We will not assume that
stronger written work necessarily means better independent judgement. The findings will inform
policy teaching and the responsible design of tools such as policymemo.ai.

Why have I been chosen?
You are invited because you are a postgraduate student taking IIPP0010 and belong to one of its four
seminar groups. All students can attend the same teaching and use the normal learning resources,
whether or not they participate in the research.

Do I have to take part?
Taking part is entirely voluntary. It will not affect your marks, teaching, feedback or relationship
with staff. You may use policymemo.ai and take part in every seminar whether or not you join the
study. Where a seminar uses AI, an equivalent peer-feedback route is available if you prefer not to
use the tool. You may agree to some forms of research use and decline others.

Research consent and participant contact will be managed by Naeha Rashid, independently of your
seminar tutor or marker. Interviews will be conducted by Christopher Rosselot, Juan Pablo Carbonell
Muñoz or Maria Nieto Rodriguez, not by Jack Strachan. Arrangements will keep your research
participation and interview responses from the person marking your work until marking is finalised.

You will be asked to make separate choices about the parts of the study to which you consent using
this Microsoft Form: https://forms.cloud.microsoft/pages/responsepage.aspx?

request withdrawal without giving a reason by contacting Naeha Rashid at naeha.rashid@ucl.ac.uk.
Requests to remove data that can still be linked to you can be made until 30 June 2027. Once
information has been irreversibly anonymised or incorporated into published findings, it may no
longer be possible to remove it.

What will happen to the results of the project?
We plan to publish findings in academic journals, reports, conference presentations and blog posts.
Anonymised research findings, not identifiable work or interview records, may also inform
improvements to policymemo.ai. Short quotations may be published only where you separately
permit this and the research team judges that you cannot reasonably be identified through the
quotation or policy topic. A summary of findings will be shared with participants and the module
team.

What will happen to me if I take part?
Research activities are planned between 27 November 2026 and 30 September 2027, with interviews
and any later policy task planned after the release of final grades and by 31 May 2027. With your
consent, the study may also use work you completed earlier in the module, such as your formative
assignment. All the activities described below are optional research contributions; normal teaching is
available to everyone.

Seminar work. You will continue working on your Personal Policy Problem alone and with peers as
part of normal teaching. Two seminar activities are planned to include a specific policymemo.ai
critique (Moore’s Strategic Triangle and Thinking Hats peer review), alongside other seminars
without required AI use. With your consent, researchers may use copies of selected written work
created in normal teaching. Choosing whether to contribute research data will not affect these
activities.

Formative assignment and tutor feedback. You may separately permit the study to use (1) your
original formative policy problem definition and analysis, and (2) the written feedback your tutor
gave you on that assignment. These are two independent choices. If you agree, Naeha Rashid will
arrange for the materials to be securely supplied or released through UCL-approved systems, and

Will I be identifiable?
We do not plan to identify you in research reports or publications. Even when names are removed, a
distinctive Personal Policy Problem or quotation may make someone recognisable. We will check for
this risk and change or remove identifying details before using any quotation. Quotations will be
used only with your separate permission and where the team judges that identification is unlikely.

In a small seminar group, people familiar with your work may recognise your policy topic. We will
seek to minimise this risk. If information suggesting serious wrongdoing or potential harm is
disclosed, UCL may have obligations to contact relevant services or statutory agencies in accordance
with applicable procedures.

Will I be recorded and how will recordings be used?
Audio recording of interviews is optional and requires separate consent. If you agree, audio
recording will take place using a UCL Microsoft Teams account, with cameras switched off. If you do
not agree to audio recording, the interviewer will use written notes. No video recording of teaching
sessions is planned. Recordings, notes and transcripts will be kept in access-controlled UCL OneDrive
or SharePoint and will be available only to authorised researchers.

Research material and consent/contact records will be stored in access-controlled UCL OneDrive or
SharePoint. Independent staff will hold consent records and the key linking participant codes to
names separately. Jack Strachan and David Eaves will analyse only pseudonymised material after
marks are finalised. Research materials may include consented formative assignments, tutor
feedback, seminar work, selected policymemo.ai conversations, final memos, observation notes,
interviews and follow-up tasks. Identifiable research material will not be stored on personal devices
or in the policymemo.ai repository.

How long will the data be kept? Identifying records will be deleted by 31 March 2028, and
anonymised research data by 30 September 2032.

Potential use of AI in research analysis. Human researchers will analyse the material first. An
institutionally authorised AI tool may be used to challenge genuinely non-identifying, researcher-
written summaries or analytical propositions where UCL permits this. Student writing, interview
transcripts or other participant material, even with names removed, will not be uploaded to an AI
service for research analysis unless the specific service, data types and safeguards have received the
necessary institutional authorisation and have been explained to participants. An LLM will not
determine your marks or replace human researchers.

What are the possible disadvantages and/or risks of taking part?
Your Personal Policy Problem may draw on personal or workplace experience. Coursework, tutor
feedback, interviews and selected AI conversations could contain sensitive information or details that
indirectly identify you. Please do not enter personal, confidential workplace or third-party
information into policymemo.ai. Before sharing material for research, remove anything you do not
wish to include. You may skip questions, pause or stop interviews, and decline the later exercise.

policymemo.ai runs inside an AI tool (Claude, Microsoft Copilot or Google Gemini) under that
provider’s own terms. Choosing to use it for teaching is different from consenting to share your
conversations for research. If you would rather not use an AI tool, you can complete the same
seminar activities through peer review.

Jack developed policymemo.ai and teaches on IIPP0010. We recognise that students may feel pressure
to give positive feedback about a tool developed by their tutor. Independent staff will manage
consent and during-module interviews, and your marker will not know who took part before
assessment is final. Critical views are as useful as positive ones.

If participation causes distress or discomfort, you can stop an interview or activity, skip questions or
request withdrawal by contacting Naeha Rashid (naeha.rashid@ucl.ac.uk). You can request removal
of data still linked to you until 30 June 2027. Once material has been irreversibly anonymised or
included in published findings, it may no longer be possible to remove it.
