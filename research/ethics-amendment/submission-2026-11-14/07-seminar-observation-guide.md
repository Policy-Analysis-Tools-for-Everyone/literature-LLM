Practice as software
Structured seminar observation guide
Seminars: Moore’s Strategic Triangle and Thinking Hats
Purpose: To examine how students make, challenge and justify policy-analysis decisions through interactions
with peers and policymemo.ai.

 Session date: __________________         Seminar: __________________              Observer: __________________

1. Students’ initial reasoning
What position, assumption or policy decision are students working with? Where observable, note how they justify
their initial thinking, including their use of evidence, recognition of uncertainty and consideration of policy trade-
offs.

2. Challenges to their reasoning
What challenges or alternative perspectives emerge through peer discussion and policymemo.ai? Where possible,
distinguish what comes from peers, the tool and the student’s own thinking.

3. Evaluating feedback and alternatives
How do students assess the suggestions and challenges they encounter? Look for instances where students
question evidence, test assumptions, compare competing criteria or explain why particular advice is useful or
unconvincing.

4. Making and justifying decisions
What do students decide to retain, reconsider, adapt or reject? What reasons do they give? Are they able to
explain why their eventual position is more defensible, or why their original position remains appropriate?

5. Seminar-specific observations
Strategic Triangle: How do students navigate tensions between public value, legitimacy and support, and
operational capacity? Does policymemo.ai introduce new considerations, and how do students judge their
relevance?
Thinking Hats: How do students respond to successive and potentially conflicting critiques? How do they decide
which peer or AI suggestions to incorporate, adapt or reject?

Observation notes
For one or two significant policy decisions, where observable, record:

 Initial position or decision

 Challenge or alternative introduced, and by whom

 Student’s response and reasoning

 What changed or remained unchanged

 Other possible explanations or missing information

Guidance for observers
Record concrete examples of what consenting students say and do, distinguishing observations from
interpretations. Do not assume that revising an argument demonstrates better judgement, or that agreeing with
AI demonstrates dependence.
Observations should be limited to consenting participants under approved arrangements. No audio or video
recording is planned, and observers should not intervene in the activity.
The observations provide evidence of reasoning during the seminars, rather than proof of independent learning
or development over time.
