Practice as software

Data management and analysis protocol
PROPOSED FOR ETHICS REVIEW | 14 November 2026

1. Scope and research materials
This small longitudinal qualitative study examines how postgraduate students make, question and justify
policy decisions while working independently, with peers and with policymemo.ai. Data will be used
only for research activities and materials to which the participant has separately consented.

Potential materials are: original formative problem analyses; written tutor feedback; selected seminar
work; voluntarily shared policymemo.ai conversations; final Personal Policy Problem memos (after
marking); consented structured observation notes; two optional interviews; and an optional post-module
individual policy task. Consent to one source does not authorise access to another. No academic marks
are used as research outcomes.

2. Consent, collection and access
Naeha Rashid (naeha.rashid@ucl.ac.uk) manages consent, participant contact and withdrawal requests
through the designated Microsoft Form and authorised UCL systems. Christopher Rosselot, Juan Pablo
Carbonell Muñoz or Maria Nieto Rodriguez conduct research interviews. Jack Strachan does not
interview participants or receive participation identities or attributable research materials before
marking is finalised.

Formative submissions and tutor feedback are separate optional research sources and will not be
retrieved automatically. Students choose which tool conversations to share and may remove material
beforehand. Observation notes will cover only consenting participants; if a mixed-consent discussion
cannot be separated, no attributable research notes will be collected from it. Interview audio recording
using a UCL Microsoft Teams account is separately optional; written notes will be taken if recording is
declined.

3. Storage, confidentiality and withdrawal
Consent records and contact details will be held separately from coded research material. Authorised
staff will assign participant codes and retain the re-identification key under restricted access. Research
files, including Teams recordings and transcripts where consented, will be stored in access-controlled
UCL OneDrive or SharePoint, not on personal devices or in the policymemo.ai GitHub repository. Jack
Strachan and David Eaves will analyse pseudonymised materials only after marks are finalised.

Participants may stop participating at any time and request removal of material still linked to them until
30 June 2027 by contacting Naeha Rashid. Removal may no longer be possible once material has been
irreversibly anonymised or incorporated into published findings. Identifying records will be deleted by
31 March 2028 and anonymised research data by 30 September 2032, consistent with the accompanying
Participant Information Sheet.

4. Human-led qualitative analysis
The research team will compare participants’ reasoning across their formative work, feedback, selected
seminar activities, AI interactions, interviews and later work where those materials are available.
Analysis will examine five connected areas: problem framing; use and evaluation of evidence; treatment
of uncertainty; consideration of options and trade-offs; and the justification of judgements.

Researchers will distinguish what is directly visible in a document or conversation from students’
retrospective accounts and from researcher interpretation. They will examine why students accept,
adapt, reject or retain suggestions, including cases in which agreeing with AI is defensible or changing an
answer is not. Attention will be paid to peer and tutor contributions, prior AI use, task differences, and
other explanations for apparent change. A second human researcher will review a purposive sample of
interpretations, with disagreements and subsequent decisions documented. The study will not treat
better AI-assisted writing as proof of independent learning or infer that policymemo.ai caused any
observed change.

5. Limited LLM-supported critical review
Human researchers will complete the initial analysis. Subject to UCL authorisation, an institutionally
permitted LLM may then be used to challenge genuinely non-identifying researcher-written summaries
or analytical propositions—for example, by identifying weak inferences, alternative explanations,
contradictions or negative cases worth examining.

The model will not make primary coding decisions or assess participants. Student work, transcripts,
observation notes or quotations will not be entered into an AI service solely because names have been
removed. Any proposed processing of participant-derived material would first require specific ethics,
information-security and data-protection approval for the service and data involved, together with the
necessary participant information and permissions. If that is not authorised, the analysis will remain
fully human-led.

Any permitted LLM review will be logged, including the purpose, approved service, nature of input,
model suggestions and human decisions. Researchers will verify proposed revisions against the protected
original data; model output will not be treated as independent human validation.

6. Reporting and dissemination
Findings may be reported in academic papers, reports, conference presentations and blog posts. Short
quotations will be used only with separate permission and after checking whether wording or a
distinctive policy topic could identify a participant. Non-identifying aggregate findings may inform
policymemo.ai development; individual participant materials will not be shared for that purpose. The
analysis will report patterns and contrasting cases without presenting the small, self-selected sample as
statistically representative.
