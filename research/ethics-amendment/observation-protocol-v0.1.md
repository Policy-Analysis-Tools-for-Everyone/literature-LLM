# Structured seminar observation — draft protocol v0.1

**Status:** new research method requiring amendment. Original approved plan only permits **non-identifying, group-level teaching notes**, not consented individual observation records.

## Proposed setting

Two core PolicyMemo.ai sessions: Strategic Triangle and Thinking Hats, after their relevant teaching modifications have been approved for study purposes. The existing formative-feedback and other seminars can provide contextual non-AI teaching activity **without automatically becoming research observations**.

**Observer:** independent or trained member of research team [to confirm]. **No audio/video recording by default.** Distinguish observations permitted by consent from ordinary teaching and ensure safeguards for mixed-consent groups.

## Observation sheet

| Field | What to note |
|---|---|
| Session context | Date, activity, topic, group size, observer, which AI tool (if any), whether available to all, prompt/task conditions |
| Permitted unit of observation | Consenting participant/group only; method to prevent capturing a non-consenter's words or identity |
| Significant policy choice | A problem frame, causal claim, evidence interpretation, uncertainty, policy criterion, option or trade-off the students discuss |
| Contributions visible | A peer challenge, tutor prompt or AI critique **only when genuinely observed**; unknown contributions remain unknown |
| Reasons offered | Evidence, values, legitimacy, feasibility or uncertainty used to defend a position |
| Response | View retained, revised, rejected or deferred, with or without visible reasons |
| Notes about tool use | Whether AI critique was requested, how it was introduced, whether participants examined its reasoning |
| Context | Time pressure, task rules, prompts that specifically require justification, group interaction |
| Contradiction or alternative explanation | Did peer or tutor feedback already make the same point? Did the activity itself prompt the response? |
| Evidence confidence | Directly seen/heard; later reported; observer interpretation; insufficient evidence |

## Procedure

Before the session, identify the consenting population from the independent contact **without giving the tutor an identifiable participation list**. If mixed-consent groups cannot be observed without collecting non-consenters' contributions, **do not collect attributable research notes from those groups**. Agree a workable arrangement with UCL before claiming observations are feasible.

During teaching, observe without directing the students' reasoning; collect concise descriptive notes, not assumed thoughts. Afterward, separately note analytic hypotheses and negative cases. Obtain links to eligible worksheets or tool transcripts only by independently approved submission routes.

## Quality controls

Pilot the observation form with a **synthetic role-play**, then have the human researchers discuss ambiguous examples. Collect no identifying participant data on personal devices. Link to optional interviews only after approved consent/custody processes. The researcher-teacher must not access participation information before grades are final.

## Ethical and interpretive limits

Observation can illuminate how peer challenges, teaching prompts and AI are used, but **cannot read private cognition** or establish longer-term independent skill. Whole-room plenaries and contributions from non-consenting students should be recorded only as permitted anonymous/general teaching context, not attributable individual research data.
