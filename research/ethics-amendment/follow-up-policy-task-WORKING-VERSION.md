# Practice as software — Follow-up individual policy-analysis task
**WORKING VERSION FOR ETHICS REVIEW — example research instrument, subject to refinement before use**

**Proposed duration:** approximately 15–20 minutes  
**Format:** optional, individual, ungraded, without AI or external research in the initial response  
**When:** after the module and release of final grades, for participants who separately opt in

## Introduction for participants

This exercise explores how you approach an unfamiliar policy problem and make decisions with limited information.

There is no single correct answer. We are interested in how you interpret the problem, evaluate the available evidence and explain your reasoning.

Please complete the exercise independently, without AI or external research. You can write in brief paragraphs or notes. You are not expected to produce a policy memo.

## The policy challenge: Improving access to skills programmes

A local council is concerned that many young adults enrolling in publicly funded skills and employment programmes do not complete them.

Last year, **400 people enrolled, but 148 left before completing their programme**.

The council has gathered some initial evidence:

- **Participant survey:** Of 64 people who responded to a voluntary survey, 31 mentioned transport difficulties, 26 mentioned inconvenient programme schedules, and 19 mentioned difficulties navigating programme requirements. Respondents could identify more than one problem.
- **Programme providers:** Several providers believe that more flexible teaching hours would improve attendance, although they have not tested this.
- **Local employers:** Employers have suggested that unreliable evening transport makes it difficult for young people combining training with work.

The council has **£150,000** available for a six-month pilot.

Two options have been proposed:

- **Option A:** Introduce transport support for programme participants, with an estimated pilot cost of **£140,000**.
- **Option B:** Fund more flexible programme hours and improved support from training providers, with an estimated pilot cost of **£85,000**.

Neither approach has been evaluated locally, and the council does not yet know which barriers are most important.

## Your task

Imagine you have been asked to advise the council.

1. **How would you define the policy problem?** What do you think might be causing it? Are there assumptions in how the council has framed the issue?
2. **What do you make of the available evidence?** Which information would you give weight to? What remains uncertain, and what additional evidence would help?
3. **How would you approach the decision?** Consider the proposed options and explain what you would recommend the council do next. You may suggest a different approach if you think it is more appropriate.
4. **What is your reasoning?** Explain why you favour that approach, what trade-offs you have considered, and what might lead you to reconsider your recommendation.

## After the exercise

The interviewer may invite you to discuss how you approached the problem, which decisions you found difficult, and whether or how AI might have contributed to your analysis. The task is ungraded and separate from the interview; you may decline it even if you agree to be interviewed.

## Researcher note — not part of the participant exercise

**This is an illustrative working version for ethics review, not a final or validated assessment instrument.** The council, statistics, survey and options are fictional. The example demonstrates the anticipated task content and burden; wording, scenario and comparability may be refined and submitted for any further review required before field use.

**Research purpose:** examine how students define an unfamiliar problem, scrutinise incomplete evidence, identify uncertainty, compare alternatives and justify a provisional choice **without immediate AI support**. The task complements the second interview, which captures participants' own reflections.

**Limitations:** a single task cannot establish that PolicyMemo.ai caused any change in independent judgement. The original formative assignment addresses a student's own problem and is not directly equivalent to this timed fictional case. Do not describe these as matched pre/post tests or score revision quantity as learning. Research access, consent, timing, data handling and any changes to the working instrument remain subject to the approved amendment.
