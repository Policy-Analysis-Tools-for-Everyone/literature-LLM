# Data management and qualitative analysis — proposed amendment v0.2, human first

**Date:** 14 November 2026. **Status:** draft proposed processing plan, not a permission or security authorisation. The **original approved plan prohibits putting student research data into any AI analysis system**.

## A. What data is being proposed

| Data class | Collector / source | Research purpose | Currently permitted by original plan? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary seminar written work (opt-in) | Independent administrator / approved submission | Initial and later positions, rationale and revision | Yes, under original optional consent and handling conditions |
| Original submitted **formative policy problem definition and analysis** | Independent approved retrieval or voluntary submission; separate consent | Document early problem framing, evidence and causal reasoning, before PolicyMemo.ai-specific teaching | **Not explicitly authorised as a separate assessment record — amendment** |
| **Tutor's written formative feedback** | Independent approved retrieval or voluntary submission; **different consent** | Document an external critique and interpret reported feedback uptake | **Not explicitly authorised — amendment; verify staff feedback processing rights** |
| Voluntarily shared selected, redacted PolicyMemo.ai conversations | Student chooses what to share | Document what AI suggested and visible responses | Yes, under original optional consent |
| Final Personal Policy Problem memo | Approved consent; **access after marks final** | Continuity of policy argument | Yes, with separate consent and post-marking access |
| Non-identifying group-level teaching notes | Tutor as ordinary teaching context | Broad teaching process | Yes, not named individual observations |
| New structured consented classroom observation | Trained independent observer | Peer, teacher and tool contributions visible in seminar discussions | **No — amendment** |

## B. Human-led analysis protocol

1. Store all research originals, signed/online consent, recordings and identification key only in a specified **UCL-approved, access-controlled** location. Separate contact identity and material from the tutor/marker until grades final. Use unique participant codes, keep dates and permissions metadata, and maintain a data inventory with lawful retention/withdrawal limits.
2. Keep the **original formative script and written feedback as separate dated objects**. Compare the starting reasoning with what feedback actually recommended, and distinguish those recommendations from whether students said or showed they had acted on them. Document any reported prior use/non-use of general AI tools, but do not infer AI use from writing style.
3. Read each consented text and describe **what is visible**, distinguishing the student's words, researcher interpretation and unobserved group influences. Analyse the original five dimensions: problem framing, evidence, uncertainty, options/trade-offs and judgement/justification.
4. Identify significant policy decisions and any revisions, including **justified non-revision**. Compare tutor feedback, peer critique, agent suggestions and later work **only to the extent the sources permit**; don't infer who originated an idea from a polished final argument.
5. Develop and refine themes through **within-student** and **cross-student** comparison, with negative cases (defensible acceptance of AI, poor rejection of good advice, better work without a discernible rationale, reflection without change) and relevant contextual differences.
6. A second **human** researcher re-analyses a purposive sample and logs both agreements and disagreements; do not mislabel two LLM passes as independent human coders. Keep audit trail for changes to definitions and conclusions.
7. Distinguish assisted task quality from the exercise of judgement and from evidence of independent development. Do not claim causal effects from a small nonrandomised teaching study.

## C. Limited LLM critique: proposed workflow and decision gate

**Baseline mode (available without exposing participant material to AI):** humans prepare genuinely non-identifying **aggregate interpretations, hypothetical examples or literature-based arguments** in approved storage. A model can be asked to **challenge the logic** of these abstract summaries: “What rival explanations remain? Which claims go beyond the stated evidence? What negative cases should humans search for?” Researchers then check suggestions **against the controlled original participant data themselves**.

**Conditional extension:** using even name-redacted excerpts, interviews or work products in a model requires a clear and **specific** institutional decision covering (i) approval under the amended research protocol; (ii) information-security and data protection review; (iii) lawful processing basis and whether participant notice/permission is needed; (iv) whether the data are actually anonymous or pseudonymous; (v) AI provider/service identity, contractual processor terms, location and retention; (vi) training/secondary-use settings; (vii) permissions to reuse students' original work; and (viii) mitigations for re-identification of distinctive Personal Policy Problem topics. Do not assume a private GitHub repo or consumer subscription is a secure/authorised university analysis environment.

**If UCL does not approve it:** analyse the participant corpus **entirely by humans**. Keep literature review, synthetic examples and public aggregate methodology criticism separate.

## D. Record of model assistance, if and only if used lawfully

| Field | Example / definition |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Challenge the human's preliminary interpretation, not make primary coding or grade decisions |
| Service/model | Exact approved deployment and version |
| Inputs | Data classification, anonymisation and authorisation record; never copy participant data into this public/private documentation repo |
| Prompt | Research question posed to the model, saved in approved storage |
| Outputs | AI-suggested contrary reading, missing-case prompt, or logical flaw |
| Human review | Accepted / rejected / uncertain, and why; source verified against restricted originals |
| Contribution | Actual resulting analysis change, if any |
| Bias/limitations | Potential overgeneralisation, invented interpretations, sensitivity to prompt and confirmation bias |

**No claim that LLM critique improves quality without a bounded evaluation of the suggestions.**

## E. Ethics and data-protection checks before submission

- Confirm current approved PIS/consent documents, project identifier, actual UCL retention rules and secure recording/transcription tool.
- Confirm whether **legitimate institutional research processing** relies on consent as the legal basis or a separate lawful basis; participation consent and GDPR lawful basis are not necessarily the same legal concept. Do not amend this by assumption.
- Align withdrawal deadlines with both follow-up interviews and re-identification/key destruction.
- Avoid collecting third-party or workplace data; advise participants not to share them with PolicyMemo.ai.
- Ensure data cannot inadvertently be linked to academic marks or tutor/marker participation decisions.
- Obtain current UCL ethics and information-security/data-protection guidance for the **specific** tool and data classification; the original blanket prohibition remains in force until formally replaced.
