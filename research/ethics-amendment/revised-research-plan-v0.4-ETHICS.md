## Research purpose and question

This study investigates how postgraduate students make policy decisions when working independently, with peers, tutors and an AI policy-analysis tool, policymemo.ai. It focuses on the reasons students give for choices about problem definition, evidence, uncertainty, alternatives and trade-offs. The study also explores whether students' approaches appear to change across the module and in later work, without assuming that an improved AI-assisted answer demonstrates improved independent judgement.

**Research question:** When and how do students make, scrutinise and justify consequential policy-analysis choices in AI-supported practice, and what evidence, if any, suggests change in that capability over time?

The analysis is informed by research on policy judgement, evaluative judgement, metacognition, reflective practice and situated professional practice. These perspectives guide interpretation rather than operate as a set of psychological measures.

## Setting and participants

The study is embedded in **all four seminar groups** of IIPP0010 at UCL's Institute for Innovation and Public Purpose. Students are adults enrolled on a postgraduate programme. Research participation is optional; everyone can attend the same teaching and use policymemo.ai whether or not they consent. Equivalent peer-feedback routes are available to students who prefer not to use AI.

The study is a **small longitudinal qualitative investigation**, not a randomised comparison of AI and non-AI students. A purposively selected, consenting subgroup of **approximately six to eight students** will be invited to participate in two interviews. No claim of statistical representativeness or a causal effect of the tool will be made.

## Teaching activities and sources of evidence

The teaching will include **two core opportunities for a structured policymemo.ai critique**. In Moore's Strategic Triangle, students first consider public value, legitimacy and operational capacity independently and with peers, then may ask the tool to challenge their reasoning. In Thinking Hats peer review, students receive several different peer perspectives on their developing policy argument before considering a bounded AI critique. Other seminars, including the formative-feedback activity, remain primarily peer- and tutor-led. Students can retain, modify or reject advice, with reasons; revision is not treated as intrinsically better judgement.

Subject to separate permission, the research may use selected normal seminar work and voluntarily shared policymemo.ai conversations. It will also seek **independent optional consent for two distinct existing materials**: the student's original formative policy problem definition and analysis, and the written tutor feedback on that assignment. These will be supplied or released through an authorised UCL process, not automatically retrieved from teaching systems. The formative precedes planned policymemo.ai activities but is **not assumed to be AI-free**: interviews will ask whether and how students used ChatGPT, Claude or other AI tools, or chose not to. Their accounts of earlier tool use will not be treated as verified records of past interactions.

The final Personal Policy Problem memo may be included with its own consent **only after marks are finalised**. A new structured observation method is proposed for selected seminars, recording how consenting students discuss evidence, encounter peer or AI challenges and justify decisions. Observers will record factual examples rather than infer unspoken thinking. Where mixed-consent group contributions cannot be separated, no attributable research observation will be collected. **No audio or video recording of seminars is proposed.**

The exact seminar delivery dates will be checked against the start of the amended approval. Activities occurring before approval cannot be represented as prospectively observed research sessions.

## Interviews and follow-up

An independent researcher will interview approximately six to eight students **during the module** for 20–30 minutes, exploring the formative assignment, original approaches to policy analysis, tutor feedback, previous AI use or non-use, and experience of policymemo.ai. A second, **30–45-minute interview after final grades have been released** will explore subsequent working practices, continued AI use, examples of judgement under uncertainty and the participant's account of change or continuity.

Interviews will be conducted by **Christopher Rosselot, Juan Pablo Carbonell Muñoz or Maria Nieto Rodriguez**, not Jack Strachan. Interview audio recording using a UCL Microsoft Teams account will be separately optional; if declined, the interviewer will use written notes. Participants may skip questions or stop. A further, **separately optional 15–20-minute individual task** on an unfamiliar fictional policy problem is proposed after the module and marking. Its student-facing example is marked **Working Version for Ethics Review**. The task is ungraded and completed initially without AI. It provides an opportunity to observe later reasoning, but is **not a matched test** of the longer formative assignment and cannot alone establish AI-caused learning.

Follow-up interviews and tasks are planned **after grades are released and by 31 May 2027**, with research activity concluding by **30 September 2027**.

## Recruitment, consent and safeguarding assessment independence

**Naeha Rashid** manages the Microsoft research consent form, recruitment contact, coded participant identifiers and withdrawal requests. Students consent separately to seminar work, formative submission, written tutor feedback, chosen AI conversations, final memo, observations, each interview/contact option, the later task, optional recording and anonymous quotations. Consent to one element does not require consent to another. Marking is unaffected by any research decision.

Identities and interview responses are withheld from the tutor/marker until grades are finalised. Jack does not conduct interviews. Research materials are accessed by the research team for analysis only after marking, under approved procedures. Participation is not visible in grading decisions. All students receive the same educational opportunities regardless of research consent.

Participants may discontinue involvement at any time and request removal of material still attributable to them **up to 30 June 2027**, by contacting Naeha. Material irreversibly anonymised or already integrated into published findings may not be retractable.

## Data management and analysis

Consent and contact information, the identity key and coded research material are stored separately with restricted access on authorised **UCL OneDrive or SharePoint**. Interview audio and transcripts, where consented, will be held in those systems rather than personal devices or the policymemo.ai GitHub repository. Identifying records will be deleted by **31 March 2028**; anonymised research data by **30 September 2032**.

Human researchers will analyse consented evidence across **problem framing, evidence, uncertainty, options/trade-offs and judgement/justification**. Analysis will compare initial formative reasoning, feedback received and reportedly acted on, observed challenges, selected AI interactions, final work and follow-up evidence where available. Contradictory cases and alternative explanations—teaching, peers, prior experience, self-selection, assignment differences—will be retained. A second human researcher will review a sample of interpretations.

**Human analysis remains primary.** Only if permitted by UCL may an institutionally authorised LLM critique genuinely non-identifying researcher-written summaries or analytical propositions to suggest gaps and competing interpretations. Participant documents, interview transcripts or selected quotations—even after names have been removed—will **not** be submitted to an AI service without explicit additional ethics, data protection and information-security authorisation for the service and data involved. Researchers will verify all AI suggestions against the protected material and record decisions; human-only analysis remains possible.

## Research outputs and limitations

Findings will inform policy education and responsible AI-tool design and may be published in scholarly articles, reports, presentations and blog posts. Anonymised quotations will be used only with separate permission and a disclosure-risk check. Aggregate findings, not identifiable participants' work, may inform improvements to policymemo.ai.

The study will distinguish improvements in supported writing from **judgement exercised in practice**, and from any evidence of sustained or independently transferable capability. It does not promise a causal estimate of policymemo.ai's effect. The final task is a **working example** that can be refined with appropriate ethics review before use.
