# Comparable individual policy task — design specification v0.1

**Status:** proposed new, ungraded research task requiring an ethics amendment, not an already-approved part of teaching. Intended as **optional**, separately consented follow-up after final grades.

## Why this is not simply a second interview

Interviews reveal how students explain and remember their learning. A separate task offers some **observable evidence of policy reasoning** in a new context. Neither alone establishes a causal impact of PolicyMemo.ai. The original course's final memo is an extended AI-eligible version of the *same* policy problem, so is not an independent transfer test.

## Proposed structure

- **Task A:** an **earlier individual** policy challenge genuinely comparable with Task B, completed as approved ordinary teaching or later as a prospective research task. Confirm what has actually occurred for the current cohort; **do not invent a prior baseline**.
- **Task B:** a **different unfamiliar scenario** after grades are final, of similar complexity, reading length, evidence quality, normative tensions, time allowance and instructions. Both should initially be completed **without AI and without peers**, with access conditions explicitly recorded.
- **Workload:** aim for around **15–20 minutes for each short written task** plus follow-up discussion; check participant burden in the approved information sheet. A short controlled artefact, not a full memo or graded exercise.
- **Scenarios:** choose two different public-policy areas to reduce simple topic familiarity, but ensure both require problem definition, evidence scrutiny, uncertainty, policy alternatives and public-value/feasibility trade-offs. Have an independent policy-teaching reviewer assess comparability before fielding.
- **Form:** a concise hypothetical briefing with two or three plausible but incomplete evidence statements, at least two possible options, affected groups and an institutional constraint. Avoid policy domains likely to solicit sensitive personal information. Provide all students the same available evidence per task; do not make the follow-up trivially easier.
- **Student response:** define the problem; explain which evidence is credible or missing; acknowledge one uncertainty; compare at least two options against defensible criteria; provide a provisional recommendation and explain what could change it.

**Optional second phase:** if ethically approved, after the individual unaided response, ask what role AI *might* play or let the student deliberately test an AI critique and revisit the position. **Do not combine the assisted phase score with the unaided task or label it an independent test.** If the tool is only discussed hypothetically, do not claim observed AI use.

## Analysis and comparability guardrails

Human researchers compare **the strength and appropriateness of reasoning**, not the number of changes, length of answers, 'AI-sounding' prose or correctness of one contested policy recommendation. Use the same broad prompts and documented domain-specific criteria across tasks. Where possible, reviewers analyse pseudonymised tasks without knowing temporal order, then review contextual traces. Have a second human reviewer critically examine borderline judgements and negative cases. Practice effects, maturation, other modules and selection into follow-up remain plausible explanations.

**What this may warrant:** examples of independently exercised policy judgement at two points (if truly comparable A/B), with cautious discussion of continuity or change. **What it cannot warrant alone:** that PolicyMemo.ai caused improvement or caused cognitive offloading.

## Practical stop/go decision

1. Is there an authentic **early comparable unaided task** already available with eligible consent? If not, consider a **future cohort** or limit current-cohort claims to post-module application.
2. Can the timing and new task be included in the amendment **before** data collection, and independent student contact maintained?
3. Is task burden proportionate for six to eight volunteers, and is a second coder feasible?
4. Does UCL approve the exact permitted support/recording conditions?

A field-ready scenario pack should be written and pilot-tested using **synthetic answers** only after these choices are made; this specification is enough to describe the research method at the first amendment-drafting stage.
