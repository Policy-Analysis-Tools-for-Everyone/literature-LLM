# Participant information sheet — proposed amendment v0.2

**Study:** *Practice as software: exploring the use of AI in policy reasoning and critical thinking*  
**Department:** Institute for Innovation and Public Purpose, UCL  
**Status:** **For future ethics review; not approved for recruitment or collection.** Update from the authoritative original participant sheet, not an assumption that the 2025-labelled template is the approved version.

### Why is this study happening?

We want to understand how postgraduate students make decisions about policy problems while studying with other people and using AI tools such as PolicyMemo.ai. We are interested in how students assess evidence, consider alternatives and explain what they think, and whether their approaches change during or after the module. The research may inform policy teaching and the design of learning tools. It is **not an assessment of your ability, and will not affect your grades**.

### Do I have to take part?

No. You can take part in all teaching, including the two seminars involving PolicyMemo.ai, without joining the research. An equivalent peer-feedback activity is available if you prefer not to use AI. Choosing not to take part, declining a particular research activity, or withdrawing will not affect your studies, grades or relationship with staff. You can criticise the tool freely.

### Who can take part, and how?

Adult students enrolled on IIPP0010 [**approved seminar groups and any newly authorised groups to be confirmed**] may be invited. An independent member of the teaching/research team [**name and contact to confirm**], not the person marking your work, will manage optional research consent and contact.

### What would I be asked to do?

**Teaching continues for everyone.** If you separately consent, we may use copies of written work produced in the normal seminars and/or selected PolicyMemo.ai conversations you voluntarily choose to share after removing material you do not want included. You can separately choose whether researchers may use **(a) your original formative policy problem definition and analysis**, and **(b) your tutor's written feedback on that assignment**. These are two independent choices; saying yes to either does not require agreeing to the other. The formative was an ordinary ungraded assignment with written feedback and was completed before the planned PolicyMemo.ai teaching sessions. We will ask in an optional interview whether you used other AI tools when preparing it; we will not assume you did or did not use AI. These research records will be handled by approved independent staff, and the teaching researcher will not see the data or participation identities before grades are finalised. We may ask permission to use your final policy memo **only after grades have been finalised**.

We also propose a limited number of **research-specific** activities, each with its own optional consent:
- An independent researcher observing selected teaching discussions and taking structured notes about reasoning. [**Specify observer, whether notes identify individuals, and arrangements for mixed-consent groups before approval.**]
- A first interview during the module (approximately twenty to thirty minutes), conducted by an independent interviewer while marking protections remain in place.
- A follow-up interview after final grades (approximately thirty to forty-five minutes), potentially including a separate brief unfamiliar policy exercise [**duration to be confirmed**] about a new policy problem. The second task is **optional separately**; it asks how you work through a problem, not whether you know a correct answer.
- Interviews may be audio-recorded only if you separately agree; the approved recording/transcription process will be identified before recruitment.

There will be no penalty for declining to share any particular conversation, task, document, quotation, interview or recording. The number/timing of observations and interviews will be specified in the approved final sheet.

### What information will be used?

Only items you expressly consent to share. We will not use assessment marks as study outcomes. We may ask about prior AI use or non-use, including what students used AI for when preparing the formative; these are voluntary retrospective accounts. We will avoid unnecessary demographics. A policy topic or quotation could be identifying even without a name, so we will check this carefully. Please do **not** include personal, third-party, confidential workplace or sensitive information in AI prompts or submissions. If material shared accidentally contains this, an authorised researcher will remove or restrict it in accordance with UCL rules.

### Who will see my information?

Consent and contact records will be held by independent designated staff, separate from the tutor/marker until grades are final. Researchers will analyse **pseudonymised** material in UCL-approved OneDrive/SharePoint storage with restricted access. Some material may remain indirectly identifiable in a small module. Any quotations in publications will require an additional permission and disclosure check.

### Will AI be used to analyse what I say or write?

**Currently, no participant data will be entered into an AI analysis tool under the original approval.** Researchers will analyse the material first themselves. The proposed amendment is exploring whether an institutionally approved AI service could **critically review research findings or sufficiently anonymised researcher-written summaries**, with decisions made and checked by people. [**The exact service, data types, transfer/retention terms and UCL approval are not yet agreed.**] No research data will be uploaded to an LLM for analysis unless UCL ethics and information-security/data-protection authorisations specifically permit that processing; a separate consent choice would be requested for any participant material covered by an approved pathway. Declining this use will not prevent participation in other parts of the study.

**Using PolicyMemo.ai for teaching is different from researchers sending your interview or written work to an AI tool for analysis.** PolicyMemo.ai itself may operate within Claude, Copilot or Gemini under those providers' own terms; ask us about the permitted teaching tools before use.

### Could participation cause harm or discomfort?

Discussing your work may be uncomfortable or take time. You can skip questions, stop an interview, decline an exercise or withdraw. The tutor also develops PolicyMemo.ai, which may create pressure to express favourable opinions; this is why independent consent and interviewing are proposed. The new unfamiliar task is not graded. No one will receive extra assessment credit for participating.

### How long is my information kept, and can I withdraw?

The **original plan** specifies a withdrawal deadline of **31 March 2027**, deletion of identifying information by **31 March 2028**, and deletion of anonymised data by **30 September 2032**. **These are not automatically appropriate for a newly proposed post-grade follow-up.** The final approved sheet must state the revised project dates, a feasible withdrawal deadline and the retention schedule [**confirm with UCL**]. Until that is approved, the original authorised terms govern existing participation.
