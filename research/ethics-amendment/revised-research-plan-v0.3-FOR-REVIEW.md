# Revised research plan — proposed amendment v0.3 (formative submission + written feedback)

**Study:** *Practice as software: exploring the use of AI in policy reasoning and critical thinking*  
**Setting:** IIPP0010 postgraduate policy-analysis teaching, UCL IIPP.  
**Status:** Working revision for researcher and sponsor review; **not submitted or approved**. Builds on v0.1 and a newly identified pre-PolicyMemo.ai formative assignment opportunity. The original approved plan remains authoritative until permission is confirmed.

## Purpose and research question

The study examines **when and how students make, scrutinise and justify consequential policy-analysis choices in AI-supported practice**, and what evidence, if any, suggests changes in that capability over time. Rather than treating stronger written work or more frequent AI use as evidence of learning, it investigates how students assess problems, evidence, uncertainties, options and trade-offs, and how their decisions are shaped by individual thought, peer exchange, teaching feedback and PolicyMemo.ai.

**Subsidiary questions**
1. How do students make and explain policy judgements before, during and after interaction with other students and AI?
2. What do students accept, adapt, reject or question in feedback, and what reasons support those responses?
3. How do students describe their AI working practices and the conditions under which they use or decline the tool?
4. How did students approach AI use (or deliberate non-use) when preparing their initial formative assignment **before exposure to PolicyMemo.ai**, and how does their later reported practice differ after interacting with the tool?
5. What does later individual work suggest about the continued exercise of policy judgement, distinct from better performance during the supported module?

**Conceptual framing:** policy judgement is the substantive object; evaluative judgement helps examine quality appraisal, metacognition and reflective practice sensitise analysis of monitoring/reframing, and a practice perspective attends to students, peers, teaching methods and AI. These are complementary perspectives, **not four psychometric scales**. [Saved framing](../../wiki/synthesis/theoretical-perspectives-writing-note.md).

## Design

Small **longitudinal qualitative study embedded in authentic postgraduate teaching**. Proposed recruitment remains voluntary, with a potential expansion from the original two seminar groups **only if the amendment approves it**.

**Teaching:** two deliberately bounded PolicyMemo.ai teaching activities are proposed: (A) Moore's Strategic Triangle, where AI critiques the students' account of public value, legitimacy and capacity after independent and peer reasoning; and (B) Thinking Hats memo feedback, where AI acts as a final critical reader after successive peer-review rounds. The ordinary formative-feedback seminar and other teaching remain non-AI-led. The optional third imperfect-evidence AI activity is **not a default required element**. See [two-seminar framework](../design/two-ai-seminar-framework-v0.5.md). Non-AI peer alternatives remain available to all students, irrespective of study consent.

## Formative assignment as a natural starting point

The formative assignment is the student's **initial policy problem statement and problem analysis**, submitted **before students had used PolicyMemo.ai**. It provides an authentic, dated account of how a student defined the issue, reasoned about its causes and selected initial evidence. It can anchor later interviews and comparisons with subsequent seminar work and the final policy memo.

**Proposed research materials (both agreed for inclusion in the amendment):** seek **separate optional consent** for (1) the student's **original, submitted formative problem definition and analysis** and (2) the **written tutor feedback on that submission**. Both are educational records created before this amended research plan; neither may be harvested automatically from Moodle or transferred to the researcher merely because the student joined other parts of the study. Provide an independent UCL-approved submission/release route, access the materials for analysis only **after marks are finalised**, and check any staff authorship/confidentiality considerations for feedback. The originally approved plan refers to *seminar work* rather than these separately submitted assessment records.

**Critical design distinction:** this is a **pre-PolicyMemo.ai** starting point, **not an AI-free baseline**. Some students may have used generic generative-AI tools (for example ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot), while others may have used none. The initial interview should establish **whether, when, how and why** students used those tools (or chose not to) in preparing the formative. Where recall is uncertain, code the answer as uncertain rather than infer use from writing style. Prior tool exposure, familiarity and perceived confidence can help interpret subsequent experiences, without ranking students as skilled/unskilled solely from self-report.

**Interview prompts anchored in the actual assignment** (to be incorporated into the interview guide *after this research plan is agreed*):
- “Walk me through how you developed the problem definition and analysis for your formative assignment. Where did you begin?”
- “Did you use an AI tool at any point? Which tool or tools, if you remember? At what stages, and for what purpose?”
- “What did you ask the AI to do—generate possible causes, challenge assumptions, suggest sources, draft wording, or something else? How did you decide what to keep, change or reject?”
- “If you did not use AI, was that a deliberate choice? What shaped it?”
- “Were any claims, sources or framings particularly difficult to judge? How did you assess them?”
- **After exploring the original formative practice, and only then:** “How does that approach compare with the way you work now, including any experience of PolicyMemo.ai?”

This is valuable **retrospective evidence about practice and perceived change**, but an interview cannot verify every historic AI interaction. AI chat logs from **other providers** would be a **further optional and separately governed data class**, not automatically covered by the original permission to share selected *PolicyMemo.ai* conversations. Do not request those logs until the ethics and data-handling basis is clear.

**Analytical comparison:** (i) formative problem analysis and students' accounts of general AI or non-AI use; (ii) later peer/tutor and PolicyMemo.ai challenges; (iii) final memo and the reasons students can provide for key changes; and (iv) a later unfamiliar task where ethically approved. Differences may reflect teaching, task development, feedback, experience, self-selection or a changed tool—not simply the causal effect of PolicyMemo.ai. The formative is **not automatically comparable** to a later independent problem-solving task because the task requirements differ.

**Possible research material:** separately consented **original formative assignment and, under a distinct permission, its written tutor feedback**; existing seminar written work; voluntarily shared selected PolicyMemo.ai conversations; final Personal Policy Problem memos **only after marks are final**; and non-identifying group-level teaching notes, consistent with the approved baseline. Newly collected formative assessments/feedback, interview accounts and other procedures must be clearly justified and permissioned in the amendment.

**Additional methods proposed (not authorised yet):**
- Structured, consent-sensitive observations of selected seminar activities, preferably by an independent observer, separating observed actions from interpretation.
- Purposive longitudinal interviews with approximately **six to eight adult students**, two interviews per participant: one during the module (twenty to thirty minutes) and another after grades are released (thirty to forty-five minutes, plus an optional later task). Twelve to sixteen interviews if all complete both. Sampling should include different **reported formative AI-use patterns, including non-use**, and later experiences with PolicyMemo.ai where feasible, without collecting unnecessary demographics. These are qualitative contextual variations, not a powered comparison of generic AI versus PolicyMemo.ai.
- A **short unfamiliar individual policy task** approximately six weeks after teaching, and only after grades have been released, to examine how participants approach a comparable problem under stated support conditions. An earlier genuinely comparable individual activity is needed before claiming development; if none exists, report the follow-up as later performance and reflection, not change attributable to AI.

**Timing:** the formative assignment was due on **28 October 2026** according to the 2026–27 module syllabus. The researcher states that the formative predates students' introduction to PolicyMemo.ai; confirm the actual tool introduction dates by group before calling every formative pre-tool. It must be collected only retrospectively with approved voluntary consent; this does not permit researchers to reconstruct an objectively observed pre-AI usage record. The actual seminar delivery dates and teaching/assessment timetable must be confirmed; by 14 November 2026 some proposed seminar types have already occurred. No retroactive research activity, retrospective 'baseline' or unapproved research collection is authorised by this draft. ## Research procedures and safeguards

All students receive equivalent teaching regardless of consent or tool use. Recruitment and consent are administered independently of the tutor/marker. **Collecting formative coursework and tutor feedback is voluntary and not linked to assessment outcomes; the independent custodian holds permissions, and the teaching researcher receives neither attributable work nor consent identities before grades are finalised.** **A mid-module interview requires an independent interviewer**, with transcripts and participation identities unavailable to the teaching researcher until assessment is complete, consistent with amendment approval. Participation, withdrawal and criticism of PolicyMemo.ai cannot affect marks. No unconsented students' individual speech, writing or AI prompts enter research data.

Interviews can be audio-recorded **only with a specific optional recording permission** and approved UCL collection/transcription tools; otherwise written contemporaneous notes may be used if specifically approved. Follow-up contact is separately opt-in. Avoid requesting sensitive/confidential policy or workplace data.

## Analysis

Human researchers work first from approved, pseudonymised materials in access-controlled UCL storage. Analyse **problem definition, evidence, uncertainty, options/trade-offs and justification/judgement**, allowing inductive findings and deliberate search for contradictory cases. Compare documented earlier/later positions, explanations of changes and non-changes, formative reported generic AI use/non-use versus later PolicyMemo.ai practices, AI feedback offered versus student uptake, and student accounts with classroom/artefact evidence. **Record reported tool use separately from verified traces**, and compare common reasoning dimensions rather than equating differences in formative and final assignment requirements with learning. A second human researcher reviews a purposive sample and disagreements are recorded; the small qualitative sample is not a causal or powered AI-effect trial. Do not equate improvement in an AI-supported memo with retained independent learning.

**LLM-assisted analytical critique — conditional proposal:** The original approval expressly bars putting student research data into any AI tool. Our preferred primary method remains **human-only**. We may separately request permission to have a suitable institutionally approved LLM challenge non-identifying **researcher-written summaries, analytic claims and synthetic examples**. Direct participant excerpts or transcripts, even name-redacted, would require additional explicit data protection, information-security and ethics authorisation for the particular service. A real name removed does **not** ensure anonymity. Any AI suggestion is verified by the researchers against protected originals and the accept/reject decision logged. This is not a replacement for human coding.

## Intended interpretation, limitations and contribution

A qualitative, situated account of how students exercise policy judgement with people and tools; what seems to encourage scrutiny or uncritical adoption of feedback; and whether later work/participant accounts are consistent with changing practice. Rival explanations include policy teaching, peer challenge, repeated task exposure, existing ability, language proficiency, opt-in bias, AI provider differences, assessment incentives and the researcher-developer role. The study should inform pedagogy and responsible AI-tool design, without overstating causal effectiveness or independent skill growth.

**Decision confirmed:** request both original formative coursework and written tutor feedback as separate optional research materials.
