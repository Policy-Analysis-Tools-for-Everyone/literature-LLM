# Semi-structured interview guide — two waves, draft v0.1

**Status:** for ethics review, not approved collection. **Proposed sample:** six to eight consented students, purposively varied as far as appropriate, with two interviews each. The original plan authorised **no research interviews**.

## Ground rules for both interviews

Read the approved information sheet. Remind participants: voluntary, unrelated to marks, skip questions, stop at any time, do not reveal confidential workplace/third-party information. Record only with separate consent and UCL-approved arrangements. **Mid-module interviewer must be independent of teaching/marking** and must prevent disclosure of identity/responses to the marker before grades are final.

Interviews should explore **specific examples** and invite disagreement. They are interpretive accounts, not direct observations of unrecorded thinking.

## Wave 1 — during the module (twenty to thirty minutes)

**Opening:** “Tell me about the policy problem you're working on. Which part has been hardest to think through?”

**Reasoning:** “How did you decide what evidence mattered? What uncertainty or competing values made a choice difficult?” Follow up: “What led you to that decision? What might change your mind?”

**Feedback:** “Thinking of a piece of tutor or peer feedback, which advice did you use, change or decide not to follow? Why?”

**AI use:** “Have you used PolicyMemo.ai or another AI tool while working on this? What did you use it for? Tell me about a suggestion that helped, failed or made you reconsider something.” Follow up: “How did you decide whether to trust or question it?”

**Individual/group distinction:** “What came from your own thinking, what was shaped by peers/teaching, and what, if anything, came from AI? Are those contributions easy to distinguish?”

**Ownership of judgement:** “Which part of your current argument do you feel best able to defend? Where do you still feel unsure?” 

**Critical closing:** “Can you describe a case where AI made the work harder or less clear? What have I missed?”

**Optional authorised artefact prompt:** if the participant **separately consented** and the independent interviewer can lawfully access their shared work, use a selected, non-sensitive example to ask what the participant meant. Do not show their assessment marks or press for identifiable confidential details.

## Wave 2 — after grades, roughly six weeks after teaching where feasible (thirty to forty-five minutes, *excluding* task time if separate)

**Since the module:** “What kinds of policy, study or professional work have you done since IIPP0010? Have you approached any of these differently?”

**Continued AI practice:** “Do you use AI differently now? When do you deliberately avoid it? What do you ask it to do, and what do you want to decide yourself?”

**Judgement and learning:** “Is there something about how you frame problems, judge evidence or compare options that you now do differently? Can you recall a specific example?” Follow up: “Could that also have resulted from other teaching, experience or feedback?”

**Counterexamples:** “Have you found yourself agreeing with AI when you later thought it was wrong? Have you rejected useful advice? What happened?”

**Task discussion, if separately consented and undertaken:** “What was your first understanding of this unfamiliar problem? Why did you select those criteria and supporting evidence? What alternative would have changed your provisional judgement? What, if anything, would AI help with here?” This conversation does **not** substitute for an independent task.

**Closing:** “Has your confidence or independence changed? What do you think the module/tool helped with, did not help with, or made harder? What is a different explanation for that change?”

## Interviewer memo

After each interview (without showing the tutor prior to marking): record prompt variants used, notable example, contradiction/negative case, contextual limitations, follow-up questions, and observer inference **separately** from participant statements. Do not label accounts 'learnt' or 'dependent' without evidence.

## Analytical alignment

Use five policy domains: **problem definition, evidence, uncertainty, options/trade-offs, justification**. Connect interview accounts with eligible written work, observations and voluntary AI conversations only after consent and permitted linkage are established. Two interviews reveal *accounts of changing practice*, not causal AI effects.
