# Cong-Lem & Nguyen (2026) — Towards a framework for understanding and assessing critical thinking in generative AI-enhanced learning environments

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10212-026-01089-y  
**Source:** full text supplied, 25 PDF pages. **Evidence role:** critical-thinking concepts and measurement; a *scoping review*, not a causal effect estimate for postgraduates.

## Scope
Scoping review of **29 empirical studies** from 2022–2025. Studies frame and assess critical thinking in different ways; the authors report **48%** retain a classical reflective-judgement conception, while others put more weight on AI-output evaluation and verification (PDF p. 1). Mapping constructs is not itself proof that AI improves any one construct.

## Critical appraisal
The review traces context-dependent task assessment, self-reports and psychometrics. It discusses **scaffolding** and **offloading** as competing *interpretive pathways* in the literature (PDF p. 12). The authors explicitly report **no formal quality/risk-of-bias assessment** of included studies (PDF p. 16). Their search is bounded and may miss grey literature or negative results.

## Relevance
Important caution against defining 'judgement' merely as verifying model errors. PolicyMemo.ai is interested in how learners frame problems, respond to challenge, revise positions and justify choices. These are candidate dimensions for original observational work, **not** validated outcomes derived from this review.
