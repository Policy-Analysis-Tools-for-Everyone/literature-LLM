# Dai et al. (2026) — Graduate cognitive outsourcing and behavioural sequences

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440261497399  
**Article:** *Cognitive Outsourcing in Generative AI-Supported Learning: A Behavioural-Sequence Analysis of Metacognitive Differentiation* (*SAGE Open*, 16[4]).  
**Evidence:** original graduate education research. **Population:** 90 graduate students in **18 groups; analysis conducted at group level**. **Access:** supplied 21-page PDF. **Status:** initial full-text appraisal.

## Approach and results
A one-year teaching study coded captured group interactions during AI-supported collaborative tasks, assessing the sequences and relative depth of AI use (PDF pp. 1, 6–10). Authors describe an overall trend towards more critical engagement, alongside divergent group trajectories: higher-performing groups retained checking and revision behaviours while lower-performing groups increasingly accepted generated answers with little scrutiny (PDF pp. 1, 11–16). The reported transitions are *coded behavioural proxies*, not direct brain-state measurements.

## Critical appraisal
Strength: repeated observation and sequential traces rather than attitudes alone. Crucially, the **18 groups** are the relevant analytic units, not 90 independent trajectories. Performance group labels and patterns do not identify randomised mechanisms. Group membership, teaching arrangements, task familiarity and motivation could influence the differences. The study cannot by itself establish long-term cognitive decline or an individual's lost ability.

## Contribution
The strongest lesson is **heterogeneity**—AI use and metacognitive engagement need not move in the same direction for all groups. This invites tracing episodes of critique and revision, rather than equating frequency of AI use with learning. See [synthesis](../synthesis/new-postgraduate-evidence.md).
