# Tai et al. (2018) — Developing evaluative judgement

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0220-3  
**Article:** *Developing evaluative judgement: enabling students to make decisions about the quality of work*, *Higher Education* 76:467–481.  
**Type:** foundational **conceptual/pedagogical** article, **not an AI intervention experiment**. **Source:** supplied 15-page full PDF. **Status:** conceptual full-text appraisal.

## Central argument
Evaluative judgement is the capability to judge the quality of one's own work and that of others. It includes building a contextual understanding of quality criteria/standards and **actually exercising appraisal** in situated tasks (PDF pp. 1–5; printed pp. 467–471). The authors position it as a learning/pedagogy goal extending beyond assessment marks, and examine **self-assessment, peer assessment, feedback, rubrics and exemplars** as activities that may cultivate it (PDF pp. 5–12).

## Appraisal
This is a substantive integration of formative assessment and longer-term capability, rather than new experimental evidence that any particular intervention works. The authors themselves acknowledge limited direct empirical research under this specific framework (PDF pp. 2–5). The theory does **not** require that a competent learner reject others' feedback; what matters is capacity to make defensible contextual appraisals.

## Application to the study
Use to ask *what students recognise as quality, which criteria they invoke and how they justify choices*. Treat criteria as contextual and sometimes tacit, rather than assuming all judgements are reducible to a rubric. Compare [Bearman and colleagues on generative AI](bearman-2024-evaluative-judgement-ai.md).
