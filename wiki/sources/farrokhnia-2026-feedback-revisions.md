# Farrokhnia et al. (2026) — Higher-quality AI feedback, not more revision

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00579-9  
**Article:** *Generative AI offers more, but students revise less: comparing the effects of teacher and AI feedback on student essay revisions*, *International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education* 23:6.  
**Source:** 28-page full supplied PDF. **Population:** 70 graduate students in argumentative-writing tasks. **Status:** initial full-text appraisal.

## Design
Randomised three-group comparison of **teacher feedback**, **zero-shot AI feedback** and AI feedback from **chain-of-thought-style prompting**. Students wrote an initial argumentative essay, obtained condition-specific feedback, then revised. Feedback and writing were assessed using a Toulmin-based argument-quality rubric (PDF pp. 1, 5–11).

## Results
Chain-of-thought prompted feedback was judged **higher quality** than zero-shot or teacher feedback, but this **did not translate into significantly greater revision improvements**. Teacher feedback produced comparable essay improvement despite lower feedback-quality ratings (PDF pp. 1, 12–19). The source of feedback and its evaluation must be distinguished from what students actually used.

## Appraisal
Random assignment and studying **feedback uptake in revised work** are valuable. But writing quality is not equivalent to developing transferable policy judgement; group/task setting is narrow, feedback quality and post-revision quality are rubric-dependent, and students' independent reasoning is not necessarily recoverable from the final revision. The title's 'revise less' should not be read as universal AI-induced passivity.

For PolicyMemo.ai, an agent providing stronger criticism does not automatically mean the learner evaluates or uses that criticism. **Feedback uptake and reasons for revision** deserve observation. See [scaffolding/delegation](../debates/scaffolding-vs-delegation.md).
