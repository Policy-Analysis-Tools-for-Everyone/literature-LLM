# Deng et al. (2025) — Does ChatGPT enhance student learning? A systematic review and meta-analysis of experimental studies

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2024.105224  
**Source:** full text supplied, 35 PDF pages. **Evidence role:** secondary contextual synthesis across levels of education; not direct postgraduate empirical evidence.

## Scope and methods
Review/meta-analysis of **69 experimental studies** of ChatGPT interventions published 2022–2024, examining academic, affective and higher-order outcomes (PDF pp. 1, 5–8). The review covers mixed school and university contexts; do not generalise pooled effects as postgraduate-specific.

## Findings and crucial measurement problem
The authors report positive pooled outcome effects, but explicitly distinguish learning from the quality of outputs submitted with AI assistance. They observe **nine studies permitting ChatGPT in post-intervention assessment** and **33 not stating whether it was permitted**, preventing a straightforward interpretation of improved academic performance as acquired independent capability (PDF p. 18).

## Appraisal
Strength: provides direct experimental-study mapping and unusually explicit attention to AI availability at measurement. Limitations: heterogeneity of cohorts, tasks and instruments; not an analysis of professional policy judgement; intervention and assessment visibility varies. The numbers above are authors' appraisal of included studies, not independent verification of each primary experiment.

## Use in the wiki
Serves as an **outcome-measurement caution**. Compare with [Wiles](wiles-2026.md) on assisted task quality versus immediate unaided knowledge and [Chen & Cheung](chen-cheung-learning-meta-analysis.md) on the metacognition subgroup. Do not treat this review as an additional independent experimental participant sample in pooled reasoning.
