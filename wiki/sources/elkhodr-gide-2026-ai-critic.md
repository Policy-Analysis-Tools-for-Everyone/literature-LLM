# Elkhodr & Gide (2026) — Student authority over AI feedback

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10758-026-10022-7  
**Article:** *Eliciting Student Authority over AI Feedback: The AI as Critic Mechanism in SAGE-Based Systems Analysis and Design Education*, *Technology, Knowledge and Learning*.  
**Source:** researcher-supplied 29-page full PDF. **Population:** postgraduate Systems Analysis and Design student groups in Australia. **Status:** initial full-text appraisal.

## Study design
The **AI as Critic** teaching design asked students to make unit-testing artefacts **before** an AI reviewer criticised them, then record which suggestions they accepted, modified or rejected and why. A qualitative artefact-based analysis examined **eight de-identified group submissions across five offerings**, generating **forty unit-test records** plus critique logs, literature synthesis and reflections; only seven groups completed later synthesis components (PDF pp. 1–3 and methodology).

## Findings
Students made some principled rejections of AI advice on the grounds of scope, platform constraints, risk, compliance and phasing. Other literature critiques originated in student work rather than the AI summaries. However, accessibility questions appeared when the teaching task explicitly prompted them, exposing how documented judgement can depend on **what the instruction asks students to notice** (PDF pp. 1–3; results section).

## Appraisal
This is unusually close to PolicyMemo.ai's *human-first, AI-as-hostile-reader* teaching mode. However, **group submissions are not independent competence tests**, a small selected sample limits inference, and the assessment structure intentionally elicits justifications. Visible judgement under prompts does **not** establish retained ability when prompts are removed or the competence of any individual group member. The paper explicitly acknowledges this boundary.

## Contribution
One useful method is the **accept/modify/reject with reasons** trace. We must still examine judgement prior to tool exposure, unprompted criteria and delayed independent capacity. See [scaffolding and delegation](../debates/scaffolding-vs-delegation.md).
