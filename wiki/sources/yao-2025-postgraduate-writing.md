# Yao et al. (2025) — Postgraduate metacognitive strategies in ChatGPT-assisted academic writing

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/ejed.12824  
**Article:** *A Qualitative Inquiry Into Metacognitive Strategies of Postgraduate Students in Employing ChatGPT for English Academic Writing* (*European Journal of Education*, volume 60:e12824). Accepted in 2024; publication volume 2025.  
**Evidence:** original qualitative research, 13 postgraduate L2 writers in Hong Kong. **Access:** researcher-supplied full 13-page PDF. **Status:** first full-text appraisal; researcher verification pending.

## Question, approach and observed practices
The study combines audio recordings, semi-structured interviews and ChatGPT chat logs to investigate how postgraduates manage AI-supported academic writing (PDF pp. 1, 3–5). Researchers characterise five metacognitive strategy domains: **planning, monitoring, evaluating, information management and debugging**. Examples encompass comparing draft content with intended writing goals, assessing AI suggestions, and adjusting prompts or text when outputs are unsuitable (PDF pp. 5–10).

## Critical appraisal
The sample and setting suit the postgraduate boundary, and collecting interaction traces alongside interviews is stronger than relying exclusively on attitudes. Yet logs may not represent all thinking, interview accounts can be retrospective rationalisations, the L2 writing context has particular linguistic demands, and the design does not estimate causal gains in transferable metacognitive skill. Presence of a strategy is **not equivalent** to its appropriateness or learning benefit.

## Contribution
Useful for distinguishing **student-reported intentions, observed human–AI exchanges and revised artefacts**. A methodological comparator for PolicyMemo.ai, not evidence that similar behaviours will occur in UK policy analysis.
