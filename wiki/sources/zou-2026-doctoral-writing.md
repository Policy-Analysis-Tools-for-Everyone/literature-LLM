# Zou et al. (2026) — Doctoral metacognitive experiences in GAI-assisted summary writing

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/ijal.70267  
**Article:** *Unpacking the Impacts of GAI Use on Doctoral Students’ Metacognitive Experiences in Summary Writing: A Mixed Methods Study*, *International Journal of Applied Linguistics*.  
**Source:** full supplied 15-page PDF. **Population:** 103 Chinese doctoral students. **Status:** initial full-text appraisal.

## Design and findings
Researchers compared doctoral students who **chose** to use generative AI in a summary-writing task with those who did not, using questionnaires and stimulated-recall interviews (PDF pp. 1, 5–10). The authors found **no statistically significant overall difference** in reported metacognitive experiences, while AI users reported **lower online task-specific metacognitive strategies**, alongside greater variation within the AI group (PDF pp. 1, 8–12). Qualitative interviews suggest variable use as a scaffold, affective resource and partial delegation of regulatory work.

## Appraisal
The comparison and contrast between aggregate null and dimensional differences are valuable. **Selection into AI use was voluntary**, so outcomes cannot be interpreted as the causal effect of randomly assigned AI. Experiences are task-specific, and much is questionnaire/self-report rather than directly observed thinking. The study does not establish durable independent capability.

## Contribution
An important **contradictory or qualifying case**: no significant overall difference need not imply all dimensions are unaffected. This is directly doctoral-population evidence and should temper blanket claims about AI increasing or reducing metacognition. See [cross-source synthesis](../synthesis/new-postgraduate-evidence.md).
