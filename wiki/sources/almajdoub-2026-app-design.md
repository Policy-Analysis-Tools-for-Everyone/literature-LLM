# Almajdoub et al. (2026) — Scaffold or shortcut in graduate educational app design

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci16081198  
**Article:** *AI as Scaffold or Shortcut? Metacognitive Regulation in Graduate Students’ Educational App Design* (*Education Sciences*, 16:1198).  
**Evidence:** qualitative multiple-case study of **twelve graduate teacher-education students in six UAE design teams**. **Access:** supplied 22-page PDF. **Status:** initial full-text appraisal.

## Methods and findings
The analysis combined students' educational apps, accompanying Teacher Guides and semi-structured reflective interviews. Deductive coding drew on regulation-of-cognition dimensions of the Metacognitive Awareness Inventory, complemented by inductive themes (PDF pp. 1–3 and methods/results). **Planning** was particularly apparent in written artefacts, while stronger engagement appeared where students **questioned, checked, revised or justified** AI-generated material rather than accepting it merely for convenience (PDF pp. 1, 10–19).

## Appraisal
Direct postgraduate and collaborative-design population; three data sources help expose how written justification differs from tacit thinking. But **six cases** offer situated qualitative evidence, not a causal test, and interview explanations may exceed what the artefacts independently show. The analysis partly applies predetermined categories. Acceptance of a correct AI suggestion need not indicate poor judgement, and missing documentation is not necessarily absence of a strategy.

## Relevance
Relevant to studying policy seminar teams using final artefacts, rationales and post-task accounts together. See [scaffolding](../debates/scaffolding-vs-delegation.md).
