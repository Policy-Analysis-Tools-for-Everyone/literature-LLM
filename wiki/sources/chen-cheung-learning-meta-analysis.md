# Chen & Cheung (2025) — Effect of generative artificial intelligence on university students learning outcomes

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100737  
**Source:** full-text supplied, 27 PDF pages. **Evidence role:** secondary university-level synthesis; postgraduate sample not isolated.

## Methods
Systematic review/meta-analysis of **57 studies and 97 estimations** using a moderated activity-theory/computer-supported-learning framework. Authors assess academic achievement, motivation, language skills, higher-order thinking and metacognition (PDF pp. 1, 8–11).

## Main findings
Overall reported learning-outcome effect **g+ = 0.804**, with notable heterogeneity. Crucially, the **metacognition estimate g+ = 0.078 (p = 0.789)** was **not statistically significant**, unlike authors' larger estimates for several task and attainment outcomes (PDF pp. 1, 15). This does not prove AI has no effect on metacognition in all conditions.

## Appraisal
The review's outcomes combine various interventions and instruments. It warns about short intervention durations, reliance on self-report and uncertain measurement validity; freshman and language-learning samples may drive some observed patterns (PDF pp. 15, 18–19). **Do not count its effects as independent of Deng et al.** without checking overlap among the constituent primary studies.

## Synthesis relevance
Demonstrates that AI-associated gains on measured outcomes may not correspond to detectable metacognitive gains on the measures included. Compare [Wiles](wiles-2026.md), [Deng](deng-chatgpt-learning-review.md), and [critical thinking measurement](cong-lem-nguyen-critical-thinking.md).
