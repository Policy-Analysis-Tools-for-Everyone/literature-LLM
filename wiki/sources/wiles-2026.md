# Wiles et al. (2026) — Generative AI and the temporary upskilling of knowledge workers

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-026-02566-6

**Source access:** full author-supplied/publisher PDF available in the research workspace; this note is seeded from that full text, not from an abstract alone. **Appraisal status:** initial full-text appraisal; recheck wording/pages before final publication.

**Population:** management consultants at Boston Consulting Group (working professionals; not postgraduate students).

## Question and design

Can non-technical knowledge workers complete unfamiliar data-science tasks better with generative AI, and do improvements persist when the tool is removed? In a randomised trial, 986 consultants were allocated to treatment/control, but the primary analysis uses 487 who completed assigned tasks. The treatment received ChatGPT access **and training**; controls received training to use public internet resources. Each attempted two of three approximately 90-minute technical tasks. A benchmark was created using 44 BCG data scientists. See pp. 1–3 and 5–6 of the supplied PDF.

## Measured findings

- ChatGPT-plus-training improved task scores versus control by **49, 20 and 17 percentage points** on coding, statistics and prediction tasks, respectively (pp. 2–3, Fig. 1). These are task-specific effect estimates, **not** a general 49% gain in worker capability.
- On five immediate post-experiment technical questions answered without ChatGPT, the authors found **no statistically significant treatment advantage** (p. 4, Fig. 3). This is a narrow measure; it does **not** establish that no learning occurred.
- The authors also report increased overconfidence in ChatGPT's capabilities, in an explicitly **exploratory/non-preregistered** analysis (pp. 4–5). This is secondary to our interest in judgement, not a basis for broad model-credibility research.

## Critical appraisal

Strengths: random assignment, active comparison training, preregistration for principal outcomes, expert benchmarks and separate assisted/unassisted measures. Limitations: substantial attrition between allocation and analysis; treatment combines training with access; outcomes are discrete technical tasks; immediate five-question posttest does not capture retained professional judgement; participants are highly educated consultants from one firm; parts of grading used GPT-4 Turbo with human validation (pp. 2 and 5–6). The paper discloses BCG and OpenAI employment/affiliations (p. 8); this warrants transparent interpretation, not automatic dismissal.

## Contribution to this wiki

This is evidence that **assisted task accomplishment** can expand without measured immediate independent technical knowledge gains. It **does not** establish a permanent deskilling effect or settle how professionals learn through longer-term AI-supported practice.
