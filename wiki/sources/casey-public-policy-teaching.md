# Casey (2024) — ChatGPT in public policy teaching and assessment: An examination of opportunities and challenges

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-8500.12647  
**Source:** full text supplied, 15 PDF pages. **Eligibility:** **undergraduate-only** capstone subject in a Bachelor of Public Policy (PDF p. 5). **Role:** methodological and disciplinary comparator ONLY, not direct postgraduate learning evidence.

## Design and population
A policy-brief exercise included ChatGPT drafting and student reflective essays and transcripts. **Seven of 24 students** volunteered for this research; one focus group was conducted (PDF p. 6). This is a small self-selected qualitative account within one subject, with no independent longitudinal learning comparator.

## Relevant observations
Students commented on the gap between polished policy text and analytically/politically sound policy recommendations, and reported gaining experience scrutinising AI output and constructing prompts (PDF pp. 6–9). These are student accounts and researcher interpretations, not measured gains in transferable evaluative judgement.

## Methodological relevance and boundary
Offers a precedent for analysing **reflection, policy-brief drafts and interaction traces** together, while exposing limitations of a small opt-in undergraduate sample. UCL postgraduate study design should not be inferred from this paper alone, and no new UCL observation or interview activity should be treated as ethically approved by analogy.
