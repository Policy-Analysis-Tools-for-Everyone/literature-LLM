# Gmeiner et al. (2025) — Metacognitive support agents for human–AI co-creation

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3715336.3735785  
**Article:** *Exploring the Potential of Metacognitive Support Agents for Human-AI Co-Creation*, ACM DIS '25, 26 pages.  
**Population:** 20 trained mechanical designers/engineers (mix of student/trained and industry backgrounds); **do not automatically treat all as practising professionals** without checking participant table. **Evidence:** exploratory empirical human–AI design interaction. **Status:** initial full-text appraisal.

## Design
Study of **Wizard-of-Oz voice-based metacognitive support**, with human-controlled agent probes, during a realistic generative-CAD manufacturing task. Strategies included reflective questions, planning/diagramming suggestions and expert-controlled prompting, compared with unsupported work (PDF pp. 1–2, 7–11).

## Findings
Supported participants produced **more feasible designs** overall. The authors report different benefits and costs across strategies: prompts encouraging mental simulation or sketches helped some participants articulate loads and requirements, but **questions alone were less helpful when incorrect initial assumptions were entrenched**, and some forms of support could increase reliance (PDF pp. 1–2, results/discussion sections).

## Appraisal
Highly relevant to policy design/problem-framing tools, but the intervention is **wizard-controlled**, not an autonomous deployed LLM agent; small, heterogeneous participant set and a spatial CAD task limit generalisation to public-policy decisions. Better design feasibility is not by itself a test of durable evaluative judgement. Carefully separate the *support strategy* from the technology label.

## Contribution
Useful counterexample to an assumption that asking Socratic questions is always beneficial. Tool-supported reflection needs task-sensitive guidance and visible feedback about assumptions. See [reflective practice](../concepts/reflective-practice.md).
