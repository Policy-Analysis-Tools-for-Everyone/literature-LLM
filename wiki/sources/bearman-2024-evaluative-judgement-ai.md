# Bearman et al. (2024) — Evaluative judgement in the age of generative AI

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2335321  
**Article:** *Developing evaluative judgement for a time of generative artificial intelligence*, *Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education* 49(6), 893–905.  
**Type:** conceptual article; **not empirical evidence of an intervention effect**. **Source:** supplied 14-page full PDF. **Status:** conceptual appraisal.

## Argument
Extending [Tai et al.](tai-2018-evaluative-judgement.md), the authors consider three interrelated activities: judging **AI-generated outputs**, judging **AI-mediated processes**, and **AI assessment of students' evaluative judgements** (PDF p. 2 / printed p. 893). They argue that judging quality remains a socially and disciplinarily contextual practice, not merely a binary factual correctness check (PDF pp. 3–5 / printed pp. 894–896).

## Appraisal
The paper contributes a useful conceptual map and pedagogical recommendations, **not measured proof** that the recommendations work. Its focus on output quality needs supplementing with work on how students make upstream analytical choices and how feedback is contested. It does not by itself settle the learning-versus-augmentation question.

## Implication
Study whether students **define relevant standards, evaluate the process and exercise judgement about the suitability of AI assistance**, rather than treating AI error identification as the whole of evaluative judgement.
