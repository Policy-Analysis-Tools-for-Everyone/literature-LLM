**Prepared:** 14 November 2026

## 1. The theoretical problem to be solved

The approved question contains at least **three distinct explananda**, which should not be silently equated:

1. **Exercise of judgement:** how a student currently appraises a problem, claim, criterion, evidence or policy option;
2. **Mediation of judgement:** who and what shapes the analytical choice as the student interacts with peers, teachers and PolicyMemo.ai;
3. **Development of judgement:** whether capability changes beyond the immediate supported task.

An AI-assisted policy memo can improve without establishing (3): [Wiles, C01](../sources/wiles-2026.md) and the assessment-ambiguity reviews give a boundary. An agent can shape intermediate modelling decisions without a significant adjusted direct effect on the final recommendation: [Herdağdelen, C02](../sources/herdagdelen-experts-judgement.md). More highly rated AI feedback need not produce stronger revisions: [Farrokhnia, C04](../sources/farrokhnia-2026-feedback-revisions.md). **These results motivate a framework; they do not validate its coding scheme.**

### Proposed conceptual separation

- **Outcome:** final memo/decision quality (*product*).
- **Enactment:** situated acts of framing, appraisal, revision, justification (*process*).
- **Regulation:** planning, checking, monitoring and adaptation of one's thinking (*process mechanism / sensitising theme*).
- **Trajectory:** evidence, if available, of later independent capability (*development*).
- **Conditions:** task design, prompts, tool role, peers, curriculum, prior competence and context (*potential moderators/rival explanations*).

The above are **operational distinctions proposed for this study**—not five scales validated by the review.

### Hard theoretical distinction

**Evaluative judgement** is a normative/educational *capability* about quality. **Metacognitive regulation** concerns managing cognitive activity; it may enable evaluative judgement but is not identical to assessing disciplinary quality. **Reflection-in-action** is a situated practice of inquiry into uncertain or surprising situations; it is not synonymous with any verbalised reflection. **Strategy-as-practice** changes the unit and explanatory locus to socially organised doing; it does not automatically tell us if a policy choice was epistemically or publicly good.

We should **not** treat these as four parallel subscales, nor assert that one model causally nests the other three. The empirical papers do not establish that theoretical architecture.

## 3. Three competing models to consider

### Model A — Individual evaluative capability
- **Focal question:** how well can the student assess evidence, arguments, criteria and their own/AI-generated policy work?
- **Strength:** close fit to assessment and the approved question.
- **Risk:** turns group influence and AI-mediated framing into incidental 'context' or attributes decisions to the student that were co-produced.
- **Best evidence:** within-student criterion-use and justification across work samples, plus **independent comparable task** if genuine development is claimed.

### Model B — Situated collective policy practice
- **Focal question:** how are policy choices constructed through student, peer, teacher, agent, assignments and policy-analysis practices?
- **Strength:** may explain AI influence on *upstream choices* and the teaching ecology, beyond final memo quality.
- **Risk:** moves from **development of individual judgement** to description of practice; may overclaim without access to interactions, and import an organisational strategy construct uncritically.
- **Best evidence:** sequential, consented interaction traces plus artefacts and contextual observation **if ethically authorised**.

### Model C — Capability-in-practice (preferred **working hypothesis**, not settled)
- **Focal question:** **when and how do students make, scrutinise and justify consequential policy-analysis choices in AI-supported practice, and what evidence—if any—suggests change in that capability over time?**
- **Combination:** evaluative judgement as *focal quality capability*; metacognitive regulation and reflective practice as **candidate mechanisms to test**, not proven causes; practice theory as a **context and unit-of-analysis challenge** rather than an automatic fourth pillar.
- **Strength:** directly accommodates assisted output ≠ learning (C01/C04), upstream agent influence (C02), student authority under structured critique (C03), and null metacognitive findings (C05/C08).
- **Risk:** eclecticism. To prevent it, predefine a minimal primary construct and **separate descriptions of episodes from inference about learning**. Show which observations would distinguish Model C from A or B.
- **Provisional choice:** work with Model C to design an observation scheme, retaining A and B as serious rivals until researcher adjudication.

## 4. What does 'good policy judgement' mean here?

The domain cannot be replaced by generic AI-accuracy checking. **Candidate dimensions, to contest with policy teaching sources rather than accept as a checklist:**

1. **Problem definition:** which public problem is framed, with whose perspective, causal account and boundaries?
2. **Evidence judgement:** what counts as credible/relevant evidence, what remains uncertain, and what is inferred rather than established?
3. **Criteria and public value:** which outcomes and normative criteria matter, including competing values, legitimacy, equity, feasibility and power?
4. **Alternatives and trade-offs:** what options or counterfactuals are considered and why are some dismissed?
5. **Justification under challenge:** how can a student explain, defend, amend or reject an analytical choice when faced with contrary reasons?

These echo the approved plan's existing **problem framing, evidence, uncertainty, options and judgement** dimensions, rather than silently changing its research question. They are not claims that there is one correct public-policy answer, nor fully validated indicators.

**Critical point:** a decision may be *defensible* even when it accepts an agent suggestion. Conversely a verbose response to a mandated ‘justify your decision’ prompt is weak evidence of independent evaluative capability.

## 5. Explanatory propositions that might be tested

These are **questions/hypotheses** to challenge, not observed study results:

- **P1 — Critique uptake:** AI advice is more likely to contribute to enacted judgement when the student articulates or demonstrates criteria and uses them to accept/adapt/reject, rather than merely copying text. **Rival:** the worksheet requires a rationale; effects may be prompt compliance. Links [C03](../../research/methods/ten-claim-evidence-audit-2026-11-09.md), [C04](../../research/methods/ten-claim-evidence-audit-2026-11-09.md).
- **P2 — Upstream influence:** AI can reshape policy analysis before final option choice by changing problem boundaries, evidence interpretations or decision criteria. **Rival:** AI was invited after the student already intended the change; the source experiment used artificially amplified leans. Link [C02](../../research/methods/ten-claim-evidence-audit-2026-11-09.md).
- **P3 — Regulatory variation:** students vary in planning, monitoring and evaluation; more visible AI use need not imply either stronger or weaker regulation. **Rival:** competence, language demands, group composition or task familiarity explain variation. Links [C05](../../research/methods/ten-claim-evidence-audit-2026-11-09.md), [C06](../../research/methods/ten-claim-evidence-audit-2026-11-09.md), [C07](../../research/methods/ten-claim-evidence-audit-2026-11-09.md).
- **P4 — Development is a separate claim:** present use of standards and justified revisions provides evidence of *exercise* of judgement, **not necessarily its development**. Test development through comparable individual, later and preferably less-scaffolded work if ethically available. Link [C01](../../research/methods/ten-claim-evidence-audit-2026-11-09.md); original approved study allows later submitted work **after marking**, not arbitrary new follow-up assessments.

## 6. What would disconfirm the preferred Model C?

- A student demonstrates well-founded disciplinary appraisal **without** traceable explicit metacognitive/self-reflective talk.
- Some AI-first students offer well-reasoned, independently defensible choices; some human-first students show surface compliance rather than genuine evaluation.
- Student rationales are predominantly generated by assignment wording, teacher demonstration or tool formatting rather than meaningfully chosen by the student.
- Choice provenance cannot be inferred reliably from available artefacts; our alleged ‘episodes’ are post hoc reconstructions.
- The practice framing explains collaborative activities but adds **nothing** to the actual question of capability or changes the population question inadvertently.
- The theory predicts 'developing judgement' where later individual work provides no convincing independent evidence (or none is available).

If observed, these findings should **revise or reject** Model C rather than be explained away.

## 7. Provisional conclusion and decision gates

**Working recommendation:** treat **evaluative judgement** as the focal construct for **quality-related decisions**; analyse **situated decision/revision episodes** using metacognitive and reflective indicators as provisional sensitising concepts; keep **strategy-as-practice** as an explicit lens to challenge individualistic inference, pending further original reading and fit assessment.

**Researcher decisions required** (no implied approval):
- Confirm/reject whether evaluative judgement can adequately capture *policy-specific* value trade-offs and political/ethical reasoning.
- Decide whether research question still needs **development** (longitudinal, independent evidence) or whether the existing data mainly support **exercise/justification**.
- Decide how extensively to use strategy-as-practice given this is a postgraduate **seminar** rather than an organisation's strategy work.
- Approve minimum codebook and boundaries before analysing real research material, consistent with ethics, consent and data-processing restrictions.
