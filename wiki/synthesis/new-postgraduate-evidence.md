# Current critical narrative synthesis — eighteen papers (cycle 2, 9 November 2026)

**Status:** first-pass **full-text, source-grounded** cross-paper synthesis. This cycle adds **twelve distinct sources** to [the original six-paper synthesis](six-paper-synthesis.md), giving eighteen distinct papers with wiki source notes. It does not replace the complete Scopus screening register, does not constitute systematic-review inclusion decisions, and is not a validation of durable learning. Exact claims and qualitative inferences remain open to researcher review.

## A. The strongest newly relevant evidence is postgraduate and practice-based

[Elkhodr & Gide](../sources/elkhodr-gide-2026-ai-critic.md) document eight postgraduate Systems Analysis and Design group submissions, generated under an **AI as Critic** approach: students create artefacts, then accept, modify or reject AI criticism with reasons. This makes human *authority over feedback* visible under deliberate scaffolding; it does **not establish independent or enduring competence**. Notably, accessibility considerations emerged when cued, showing that what is observed may be partly produced by the activity design.

[Farrokhnia et al.](../sources/farrokhnia-2026-feedback-revisions.md) randomly assigned seventy graduate students to teacher, zero-shot AI, and stepwise-prompted AI feedback. More highly rated AI feedback **did not result in significantly better essay revisions** (PDF p. 1 and results). Thus **quality of criticism** and **what students do with criticism** are distinct empirical outcomes. For PolicyMemo.ai, a good hostile-reader response is insufficient without studying what the student changes and why.

[Almajdoub et al.](../sources/almajdoub-2026-app-design.md) follow six graduate student app-design teams. Planning was frequently visible, while teams that questioned, evaluated and justified AI-generated material appeared to demonstrate more reflective engagement. The small multiple-case design does not show that those behaviours were caused by AI or would persist.

**Synthesis:** a human-first, AI-second exercise may create occasions for reasoning, but **observable justified revision should be distinguished from lasting evaluative capacity**.

## B. Postgraduate metacognitive practice is observable, but not reducible to five checkboxes

[Yao et al.](../sources/yao-2025-postgraduate-writing.md) studied 13 postgraduate writers in Hong Kong using recordings, interviews and ChatGPT logs. [Dai (2026)](../sources/dai-2026-uk-reading.md) examined 12 UK-based postgraduate L2 readers through interviews, reflection and supplementary logs. Both map planning, monitoring, evaluation, information management and debugging in AI-supported work. Their smaller samples and qualitative methods support **what forms of practice were observed/described**, not an AI-caused increase in metacognition or competence. Their substantial overlap in domains may partly reflect shared theoretical vocabularies, not independent discovery of the same mechanism.

[Gao & Zhang](../sources/gao-zhang-2026-doctoral-reconstruction.md) use interviews, voluntarily contributed AI artefacts and stimulated recall to propose **accountable reconstruction**: learners become answerable for AI-assisted work through verifying, adapting, explaining and defending it. Their process model is interpretive, not a causal demonstration. The distinction between *generated coherence* and *owned understanding* is compelling for policy reasoning, but should not become a normative requirement that students recreate every AI output unaided.

[Dai et al.](../sources/dai-et-al-2026-cognitive-outsourcing.md) track 90 graduate students **in 18 groups** over a year, reporting divergent sequences of active AI scrutiny and increasingly unexamined adoption. The group is the analytical unit. Group differences do not show that AI caused individuals to deskill.

[Zou et al.](../sources/zou-2026-doctoral-writing.md), in 103 doctoral students **self-selecting** AI use, find **no significant overall metacognitive-experience difference** between groups; some task-specific online strategy measures were lower for AI users. This is important counterevidence to a simple 'AI always improves metacognition' or 'AI always reduces it' account.

**Synthesis:** planning, monitoring and evaluating provide **sensitising categories**, but the study should examine **who proposed a move, why it mattered, how it was scrutinised, and what changed**. Retrospective rationales and logs disclose different aspects of work.

## C. Professional and adult experiments sharpen the boundary between performance, judgement and learning

The [original Wiles appraisal](../sources/wiles-2026.md) distinguishes assisted professional-task gains from a narrow immediate unaided knowledge check. [Herdağdelen et al.](../sources/herdagdelen-experts-judgement.md) randomise professional analysts' upstream choices, with strong effects on four intermediate choices but a **non-significant preregistered adjusted direct policy-recommendation effect**.

Newly added [Fernandes et al.](../sources/fernandes-2026-metacognition.md) report two LSAT-style reasoning studies (n=246 and n=452). In Study 2 AI users outperformed no-AI participants (mean correct **13.31 vs 9.71**) and both groups overestimated scores. **Crucial qualification:** mean confidence-discrimination AUC was **0.62 in the AI group and 0.61 without AI**—not evidence of a clear AI-caused loss of trial-level monitoring. The authors' Dunning–Kruger modelling should be interpreted as model-dependent. Study 1's historical control was **not randomly assigned**.

[Gmeiner et al.](../sources/gmeiner-2025-metacognitive-agents.md) explore Wizard-of-Oz voice-agent metacognitive support with twenty mechanical designers. Supported designs became more feasible, but **reflective questioning alone could fail when users retained incorrect assumptions**, and an agent could foster reliance. This is exploratory design-domain evidence, not an established policy-teaching intervention effect.

**Synthesis:** the emerging issue is less 'does AI help?' and more: **what is the task outcome, how calibrated is the user's understanding, and whose analytical choices determine it?** These dimensions must be separately assessed.

## D. Evaluative judgement provides a potentially coherent conceptual bridge

[Tai et al. (2018)](../sources/tai-2018-evaluative-judgement.md) define evaluative judgement as the capability to make **defensible contextual appraisals of quality** in one's own and others' work. [Bearman et al. (2024)](../sources/bearman-2024-evaluative-judgement-ai.md) extend this to evaluating AI outputs **and processes**, and what AI assessment of human judgements might afford. **Both are conceptual works**, not causal effectiveness studies.

This may better connect policy framing, evidence, criteria and trade-offs than a narrow focus on AI factual error detection. But **strategy-as-practice remains an exploratory separate lens** that we should assess from original primary texts.

## Provisional proposition for the PolicyMemo.ai study

AI-supported policy reasoning may redistribute not only cognitive labour but **the making, appraisal and justification of consequential policy-analysis choices**. Learning cannot be inferred simply from improved memos, richer AI feedback, positive self-reports or the presence of prompted justifications.

A possible analytical unit is a **decision/revision episode**: (i) who or what introduces a claim or option; (ii) what criteria are invoked; (iii) whether reasons, uncertainty, alternatives and evidence are examined; (iv) what is accepted, modified or rejected and why; (v) what participants can subsequently reconstruct or defend. This is a **proposed observational framework**, not validated coding or evidence that approved UCL data collection already occurs.

## Methodological cautions that materially change the story

- The postgraduate studies vary across **qualitative small samples**, **group-level sequential records**, and **nonrandomised self-selection**. Do not add their sample sizes as an omnibus effect estimate.
- A source may show **reasoning-in-action** without demonstrating **development over time**.
- AI feedback and student revision require separate measurement.
- A task requiring explicit defence can make competence observable *and also change what students do*.
- Contradictory/null results should remain visible.
- Existing Scopus screening and human verification are still incomplete. The eighteen-paper set is a selected working library, not all eligible full texts.
