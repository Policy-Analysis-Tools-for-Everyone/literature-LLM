# First cross-source synthesis — 9 November 2026

**Scope:** six full texts from the researcher-supplied `Narrative-Lit.zip`. This is a **provisional critical narrative synthesis**, not a final review of the 1,018 unique Scopus records or a postgraduate-specific effect estimate. The claims below distinguish *empirical observation*, *authors' argument*, and *our interpretation*.

## 1. Better assisted work and independent learning are not interchangeable

**Empirical:** [Wiles et al.](../sources/wiles-2026.md) found improved performance by professional consultants on three AI-assisted data science tasks, but no statistically significant advantage on five immediate technical questions completed without the tool (PDF pp. 2–4). The posttest is narrow and immediate, so 'no durable learning' would be too strong.

**Secondary syntheses:** [Deng et al.](../sources/deng-chatgpt-learning-review.md) note assessment ambiguity in experimental studies: nine allowed ChatGPT in the post-intervention assessment; thirty-three did not disclose whether AI was permitted (PDF p. 18). [Chen & Cheung](../sources/chen-cheung-learning-meta-analysis.md) find no significant pooled metacognition effect (g+ = 0.078, p = 0.789), even as other outcomes show positive effects (PDF pp. 1, 15). They cover overlapping research territory, so no combined numerical inference is warranted without primary-study deduplication.

**Interpretation:** A stronger memo can arise through augmentation without proving that the author has practised or developed the judgement needed to produce it independently. Yet learning may also take the form of **situated interaction with tools**, not simply unaided recall. We must decide which claims our study is competent to investigate.

## 2. AI may shape the choices that make a recommendation possible

**Empirical:** In a professional causal-analysis experiment, [Herdağdelen, Joren & Tamkin](../sources/herdagdelen-experts-judgement.md) randomised the AI agent's lean across defensible modelling decisions. The pooled flip rate for four analytical choices was 0.72 (95% CI 0.60–0.83); the **registered adjusted direct final policy shift was not significant** (p = 0.069; PDF pp. 9–10). An **exploratory in-sample** analysis found that upstream assigned leans predicted many downstream recommendations, a separate weaker form of evidence.

**Interpretation:** The fact of a human signing off a recommendation does not establish that the individual independently selected the framing, data definitions, identification strategy or criteria shaping the output. Nor does accepting an AI suggestion necessarily imply error or lost agency. **Judgement provenance** may matter as much as output quality.

## 3. Critical-thinking and metacognition constructs need precision

**Secondary mapping:** [Cong-Lem & Nguyen](../sources/cong-lem-nguyen-critical-thinking.md) document different operationalisations of critical thinking in 29 studies; some approaches reduce 'critical thinking' to checking model claims (PDF pp. 1, 12). Their scoping review does **not** formally risk-assess the primary studies (PDF p. 16).

**Interpretation:** For PolicyMemo.ai, evaluating information is only one among several analytical practices: identifying a policy problem, choosing values and criteria, reasoning about alternatives, anticipating consequences, revising under challenge and justifying a conclusion. These are **candidate observational categories**, not yet a validated coding framework.

## 4. Domain-specific teaching research helps methods, not population inference

[Casey](../sources/casey-public-policy-teaching.md) examined an undergraduate Bachelor of Public Policy capstone (seven opted into research from a class of twenty-four; PDF pp. 5–6). Reflective essays, ChatGPT transcripts and a focus group produced rich accounts of reactions to AI-generated briefs. This offers *methodological precedent* for looking beyond final outputs. **No postgraduate learning effect follows from it.**

## 5. Tentative contribution and discriminating questions

**Working proposition (researcher interpretation):** The value/risk of generative AI in complex policy reasoning may depend not only on **what work is completed**, but on **who shapes the intermediate analytical decisions**, whether alternatives are contested and how a person accounts for those decisions.

**Potential study questions, subject to ethics approval and methodological refinement:**
- Can students identify and justify consequential choices that entered their analysis through AI, peers or themselves?
- When does feedback result in evaluation, revision or reasoned rejection rather than adoption?
- How do human-first, group-first and AI-first activities change the reasoning process, and how might observing or eliciting explanations itself change behaviour?

## What is **not** established
- That AI use inevitably erodes postgraduate judgement.
- That the non-significant direct policy shift in Herdağdelen et al. was a confirmed effect.
- That two meta-analyses provide two independent non-overlapping sets of primary evidence.
- That strategy-as-practice has been demonstrated as the explanatory mechanism.
- That proposed UCL observations/interviews/questionnaires are approved or have begun.
